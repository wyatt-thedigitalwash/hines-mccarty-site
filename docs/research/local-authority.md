## 3. Directory Verdicts: SERP-Visible vs Plumbing vs Skip

**Mechanism finding (verified across 29 W1/W2 SERPs + 2 fresh checks):** the
directory URL that ranks is ALWAYS the directory's own city/practice-area
listicle page, never an individual firm profile. Firm profiles surfaced only
on attorney-name/review searches and Avvo Q&A threads. Therefore buying
directory visibility means buying position INSIDE a listicle that already
ranks, not hoping a profile ranks on its own.

Fresh re-checks 2026-07-22 (verified, non-geolocated): "best criminal defense
attorney Tampa" returned Justia #1 / Super Lawyers #2; "criminal defense
attorney Brandon FL" returned Super Lawyers #1 / Justia #2 / Avvo present,
with Curry, Matthews, Trombley & Hanes, and Mayberry as the only firm results.
The W1/W2 directory wall is stable, and no profiled competitor owns Brandon.

## 5. GBP Strategy and the Compliant Review Program

### 5.1 What winners' GBPs actually show (thin but honest evidence)

- **Category, one verified data point:** Whittel & Melton's Tampa office uses
  primary category "Criminal justice attorney" (its Spring Hill HQ uses
  "Personal injury attorney") -- verified via real-browser Maps panels in W1.
  No other profiled firm's category was observable (limitation stated).
- **Industry consensus (reported, multiple independent 2025-2026 guides:
  Local Falcon, marketingpracticality.com, everconvert, onthemap.com):**
  primary category "Criminal justice attorney", never generic "Law Firm";
  secondaries only if genuinely relevant ("DUI attorney", "Trial attorney");
  over-categorization can hurt. Recommendation for Hines & McCarty: primary
  "Criminal justice attorney", secondaries "DUI attorney" + "Trial attorney",
  contingent on a GBP-eligible Brandon address (open question 3).
- **Attributes (verified, W1):** "Open 24 hours" on both Brunvand GBPs and
  Whittel Tampa; "veteran-owned" on Whittel. 24-hour listed hours is the
  observable norm among winners (arrests happen at night). List 24 hours ONLY
  if intake actually answers; a listed-24h/unanswered-phone mismatch burns
  reviews (inferred). Set languages attribute only if Spanish capability is
  real (gates open question 8).
- **Posting cadence: unobservable for the specific Tampa winners this session
  (limitation stated).** Industry sources report most law firms never post at
  all and recommend 1-2 posts/week (reported). Given the verified low review
  bars, the inference that no Tampa winner runs a disciplined posting program
  is reasonable but unproven. Even a modest weekly cadence likely exceeds the
  local norm.
- **Q&A caution (reported, single-source):** one 2026 guide (Practice Proof)
  states Google retired legacy GBP Q&A in favor of an AI "Ask Maps"
  experience. Not cross-verified; Google rollouts are staged. Hedge that wins
  either way: publish genuine FAQ content with FAQPage schema on the money
  pages, which feeds both classic Q&A suggestions and AI answer surfaces. For
  sensitive practice areas (sex offenses, federal charges), FAQ framing is
  process/rights/discretion, never outcomes (Rule 4-7.13).

### 5.2 The compliant review-generation program (FL Bar rules)

Primary source: Florida Bar Handbook on Lawyer Advertising and Solicitation,
2025 edition, approved by SCA 12-10-25, PDF fetched and text-extracted this
research program (verified where so labeled).

1. **Asking clients for reviews is permissible (reported).** The well-known
   2011 "lawyers can't ask clients to evaluate their services" Bar News
   position predates and was superseded by the 2013 advertising-rule
   amendments; secondary compliance sources agree asking is allowed if the
   review complies with testimonial rules. The Handbook contains no explicit
   "you may ask" sentence, but its prompted-post responsibility language
   presupposes prompting is allowed. Gap: a confirmatory call to the Ethics
   Hotline (850-561-5780) would close this.
2. **Incentives are flatly prohibited (verified, two rules quoted from the
   Handbook):** Rule 4-7.13(b)(9): "the person making the testimonial may
   receive nothing of value in exchange for the testimonial." Rule 4-7.17(b):
   "A lawyer may not give anything of value to a person for recommending the
   lawyer's services." An incentivized review would also violate Google review
   policies and FTC endorsement rules (reported). The program must be
   ask-only, zero incentives, no drafting.
3. **Testimonial requirements, Rule 4-7.13(b)(9) (verified from Handbook;
   note current numbering is (b)(9), not the (b)(8) many older sources
   cite):** reviewer qualified to evaluate; actual experience; representative
   of what clients generally experience; lawyer may not write or draft it;
   nothing of value; and any review mentioning results needs a clear and
   conspicuous "may not obtain the same or similar results" disclaimer.
   Republishing a client-matter review also requires informed consent under
   Rule 4-1.6(a). Practical rules: never send suggested wording; screen out
   result-specific quotes before reposting on the site or add the disclaimer
   prominently; get written consent before republishing matter-referencing
   reviews. Competitor context, OBSERVED-NOT-RECOMMENDED (W1): Hanlon and
   Paulk both display result-quoting testimonials without disclaimers.
4. **Prompted-content responsibility, SCA Networking Sites Guidelines rev.
   2023-08-21 (verified from Handbook):** a lawyer is responsible for
   third-party posts the lawyer prompted; non-compliant reviews on pages the
   lawyer controls MUST be removed, and removal should be requested elsewhere.
   Review requests to people who are NOT current/former clients, relatives, or
   prior professional contacts can trigger the direct-solicitation regime of
   Rule 4-7.18(b) (the Handbook passage concerns page/video invitations;
   applying it to review-request texts is an inferred extension). Practical
   rule: ask only actual current/former clients via ordinary follow-up
   channels, and monitor the GBP for non-compliant prompted content.
5. **Responding to negative reviews (reported, Ethics Op. 21-1):** no
   confidential information in responses, even to deny a non-client's account.
6. **Directory-derived credentials that ARE safe for a non-certified firm
   (inferred from Rule 4-7.14):** bona fide third-party ratings with
   attribution and year (Avvo numerical rating, Martindale AV once earned,
   Super Lawyers selection once earned), Lead Counsel verification, and
   factual membership lines (HCBA, FACDL, HCACDL, NACDL, chamber). Never
   convert any of these into "expert" or "specialist" self-description, and
   never import directory listicle superlatives ("Best", "Top") into the
   firm's own titles, slugs, or copy.
