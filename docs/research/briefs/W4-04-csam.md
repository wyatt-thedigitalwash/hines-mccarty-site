**Third confirmation of the statute-string pattern, and it now generalises.**
ST-02 drug possession found that every one of eight related searches on its
informational query was a statute string or a booking-sheet string, "in the exact
register a Florida jail booking sheet or charging document uses". ST-03 sexual
battery found `794.011 8b`. This brief finds `18 usc 2252` at a higher volume
than either. Three briefs, two silos, three different statutes. **People type the
citation off the charging document, because at the moment they search, the
citation is the only vocabulary they have that they are sure is correct.**

**What that implies for structure, stated concretely because it is the whole
build.**

1. **The page is organised around the charging document, not around the charge
   name.** The reader arrives holding a document with a paragraph citation on it.
   The first job the page does is let that reader locate their own paragraph.
2. **The H2s carry the citation string in the heading text**, not only in the
   body and not only as a link. `18 U.S.C. 2252`, `18 U.S.C. 2252A`,
   `18 U.S.C. 2256`, `18 U.S.C. 3509(m)`, `18 U.S.C. 3142`, `USSG 2G2.2` are
   heading-level strings. A reader scanning for the number on their paperwork
   finds it in an H2, and a retrieval system matching the query string matches a
   heading rather than a parenthetical.
3. **Paragraph-level granularity, not section-level.** An indictment does not say
   "2252". It says 2252(a)(2) or 2252(a)(4). The distinction between those two
   paragraphs is the single most consequential fact on the page, and Section 6
   shows it is the C9. A page that answers at the section level answers a
   question the reader did not ask.
4. **The searched string is 2252 specifically**, not 2252A. The page must be
   findable on 2252 and correct about 2252A, and it must not blur them, because
   the blur is exactly the error the ranking field is making. See Section 2.

**The differentiator this brief was asked to test, and the verdict on it.** The
proposed framing was that the interaction between the mandatory minimum structure
and the receipt-versus-possession distinction has no analogue in any Florida
offence in this project's set, where escalation runs on amounts, weights, or
harm. **Tested against the operative text: it holds, and it is stronger than
proposed, because the same paragraph-level line runs through three separate
federal provisions rather than one.** It is also **not universal, and half of the
obvious extension of it fails.** Both results are in Section 6, and the failures
are recorded rather than smoothed, because a structural claim that is stated
wider than the text supports is the failure mode plan Section 4.5 exists to
prevent.

## 4. The question set a person would ask an AI assistant

Phrased the way the two real readers of this page actually talk to an assistant.
The first four are phrased the way a person holding a charging document asks. The
last four are phrased the way a family member asks.

1. "What does 18 U.S.C. 2252(a)(2) mean on my indictment?"
2. "What is the difference between 18 U.S.C. 2252 and 18 U.S.C. 2252A?"
3. "Does a federal child pornography charge carry a mandatory minimum?"
4. "Is this a Florida charge or a federal one, and can it be both?"
5. "How did the government find out about this? What is a CyberTipline report?"
6. "Why can't my lawyer get a copy of the evidence to look at?"
7. "Is there a bond hearing in federal court, or is detention automatic?"
8. "How long does sex offender registration last after a federal conviction?"

**FAQ block: PAA #5, plus assistant-questions 2, 6, and 8. Four questions.**

- **PAA #5**, receipt versus possession, is the entry point and the C9 in
  question form. Two sentences from 2252(b)(1) and 2252(b)(2).
- **Question 2** is the statute-pair question. Two sentences: 2252 is written in
  terms of a visual depiction whose production involved the use of a minor, and
  2252A is written in terms of "child pornography" as 2256(8) defines it, which
  reaches two further categories.
- **Question 6** is answerable completely and exactly from 18 U.S.C. 3509(m), and
  it is the question a family member asks that no competitor page answers. It is
  pure procedure, which is the register this page is required to be in.
- **Question 8** is answerable from 34 U.S.C. 20915 at the level of the statutory
  periods. **The answer states the three periods and states that which tier
  applies to a given conviction is determined in the case, not on a web page.**
  See the gate in Section 7.

**Excluded from the FAQ, each with its reason on the record.**

- **Question 3** is the C9 body section. Compressing the three-provision
  structure into two sentences would waste the page's single best asset.
- **Question 1** is the charging-document map, which is a table, not two
  sentences.
- **Question 4** is the forum section.
- **Question 5** is body copy. Two sentences would either omit that 18 U.S.C.
  2258A(f) imposes no duty on a provider to monitor or search, or omit the
  reporting duty itself, and either omission misleads.
- **Question 7 is excluded from the FAQ deliberately, and this is the closest
  call in the brief.** It is fully answerable from 18 U.S.C. 3142(e)(3)(E), which
  is fetched and quoted. But a two-sentence answer to "is detention automatic"
  compresses a rebuttable presumption into something a frightened reader will
  read as a prediction either way. **It belongs in body copy, where there is room
  to state that the presumption is rebuttable, that which paragraph is charged
  determines whether it applies at all, and that the decision belongs to the
  judicial officer.** That is the discretion point W4-SPEC requires the page to
  make explicit, and it cannot be made in two sentences.

**Refused outright, not merely excluded.** "What are my chances", "will I go to
prison", "can this be dismissed", "what sentence will I get", and any question
whose complete answer is a prediction. Recorded per W4-SPEC, which requires the
refusal and its reason on the record rather than silent omission.

### 6.3 Verdict on the framing this brief was asked to test

**It holds.** The proposed framing was the interaction between the mandatory
minimum structure and the receipt-versus-possession distinction, tested against
Florida offences that escalate on amounts, weights, or harm. Tested against the
operative text of 2252(b), it holds, and it is stronger than proposed because two
further provisions draw the same line. The brief was told to build on what the
statutes support if the framing failed. It did not fail, so the framing is
built on and **widened only as far as the fetched text carries it, which is the
subject of the next subsection.**

### 6.4 Where the line does not run, recorded because half of the obvious extension fails

A structural claim stated wider than the text supports is worse than a narrow
one. Three extensions were tested and **all three failed, in different ways.**

**Failure 1. It does not extend cleanly to 2252A, and that is why the C9 is
anchored on 2252.** The shape is the same: 2252A(b)(1) carries the five year
minimum and 2252A(b)(2) carries no minimum. But the three columns stop aligning:

- 2252A(b)(1) covers paragraphs (1), (2), (3), (4), **and (6)**. The 3142
  presumption list names 2252A(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(3), and (a)(4). **It does not
  name (a)(6).** So 2252A(a)(6) carries a five year minimum and is outside the
  presumption. The two columns come apart.
- 2252A(a)(7), the adapted-or-modified-depiction paragraph, has its own penalty
  subsection at 2252A(b)(3): not more than 15 years, no minimum. It is absent
  from the presumption list, and it takes base offense level **18** under
  2G2.2(a)(1), the same as possession. So there is a third tier in 2252A that has
  no counterpart in 2252 at all.

**For 2252 the three-column alignment is exact. For 2252A it is approximate.**
The assertion names 2252 and only 2252 for that reason. The page states the 2252A
departures in outline section 3 rather than hiding them, because a reader charged
under 2252A(a)(7) who read a 2252 page and assumed it transferred would be wrong.

**Failure 2. It does not extend to supervised release, and the page says so.**
18 U.S.C. 3583(k) fixes the authorised term of supervised release for "any
offense under section ... 2252, 2252A ..." at "any term of years not less than 5,
or life". **No paragraph split. No receipt-versus-possession line. Both statutes,
every paragraph, the same provision.** This was the most attractive fourth column
available and it is not there. Recorded, and the page carries 3583(k) in outline
section 11 as a fact that applies uniformly, which is itself worth a reader
knowing.

**Failure 3. It does not extend to SORNA tiering on the face of the text, and
the tier question is gated rather than shipped.** 34 U.S.C. 20911(3)(B)(iii)
places within tier II an offence that involves "production or distribution of
child pornography". Receipt is not named there. Possession is not named there.
On the text alone the line between tier II and tier I is a
**production-or-distribution** line, which is **not** the same line as the
five-year-minimum line, which falls between paragraph (3) and paragraph (4) and
puts receipt on the minimum side.

**Instruction: the page quotes 20911(2), (3), and (4) and quotes the periods at
20915(a), and it assigns no tier to any offence.** Three reasons, in order of
weight, and the shape of this gate is taken directly from ST-01's handling of
F.S. 921.0023:

1. Applying a SORNA tier to a specific federal conviction is a legal conclusion,
   not a quotation. It sits on the far side of the C12 verification allowlist in
   the same way ST-01 found the 921.0023 defaults did.
2. The tier line and the minimum line are demonstrably different lines on the
   face of the text, so a reader who saw both would reasonably assume they were
   the same and be wrong.
3. The page does not need it. The statutory periods answer assistant-question 8
   completely and honestly, and "which tier applies is determined in your case"
   is both true and the correct thing for a defence page to say.

**Recorded as verified-but-gated**, with the decision routed to the attorney read
rather than resolved here.

**How 2252 and 2252A differ, stated precisely, because this is the single most
likely thing to be got wrong.** Three differences, each from the fetched text:

1. **What each is written in terms of.** Every paragraph of 2252(a) is written in
   terms of a "visual depiction" whose "producing ... involves the use of a minor
   engaging in sexually explicit conduct". Every paragraph of 2252A(a) is written
   in terms of "child pornography", which 2256(8) defines as reaching **three**
   categories: (A) production involving the use of a minor, (B) a digital,
   computer, or computer-generated image that "is, or is indistinguishable from"
   that of a minor, and (C) a depiction "created, adapted, or modified to appear
   that an identifiable minor is engaging in sexually explicit conduct".
2. **What each reaches that the other does not.** 2252A contains the pandering
   limb at (a)(3)(B), the distribution-to-a-minor limb at (a)(6), the
   adapted-or-modified limb at (a)(7), and the enterprise provision at (g).
   2252 has none of these. 2252A also carries affirmative defences at (c) that
   turn on whether an actual minor was used, with a 14-day pretrial notice
   requirement, and 2252 has no counterpart.
3. **Where possession sits in the numbering.** Possession and accessing with
   intent to view is **2252(a)(4)** and **2252A(a)(5)**. A reader who transposes
   the paragraph number between the two statutes lands on a different offence with
   a different penalty subsection. This is why the page's map is paragraph-level.
