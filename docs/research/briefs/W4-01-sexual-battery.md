### 1.5 The terminology gap, verified rather than asserted

W4-SPEC's task direction stated that Florida has no offence called "sexual
assault" and required that the claim be verified from the statute rather than
asserted. **It was, along two independent paths.**

**Path 1, the chapter.** The chapter 794 section index, fetched from
leg.state.fl.us, lists 22 sections. The chapter is titled **SEXUAL BATTERY**.
**No section in it is named "sexual assault."** Quoted in full in 7.6.

**Path 2, the ranking chart.** F.S. 921.0022, the offense severity ranking chart,
which lists every ranked felony in Florida across ten levels. The string
**"sexual assault" appears zero times in it. "Sexual battery" appears
seventeen times.** verified by string count against the parsed chart.

**Path 3, what "assault" means in Florida when it is used.** F.S. 784.011(1):
"An 'assault' is an intentional, unlawful threat by word or act to do violence to
the person of another, coupled with an apparent ability to do so, and doing some
act which creates a well-founded fear in such other person that such violence is
imminent." **verified.** It is a threat offence, it requires no contact, it has
no sexual element, and it is a second degree misdemeanour under 784.011(2).

**Scope limit, stated rather than glossed.** These three paths establish that no
**ranked felony** and no **chapter 794 section** carries the name. A full-text
search of the entire Florida Statutes for the phrase was not run, and
unranked offences and misdemeanours outside chapter 794 were not swept.
**The claim as it should appear on the page is therefore: "Florida does not
charge an offence called sexual assault. The conduct people mean by that phrase
is charged under F.S. 794.011 as sexual battery."** That is supported. A broader
claim is not.

**This finding is body copy and deliberately NOT the C9.** See 6.4: the populated
assertion for `/white-collar-defense/wire-fraud` is already a
no-Florida-offence-by-that-name claim, and CASES-WE-DEFEND-PLAN Section 4.3's
copy-level variation requirement already names three pages opening on that move.
Making this a fourth would be the exact failure both rules exist to prevent.

### 6.2 What the adopted candidate rests on, verified

Three provisions, all quoted in full in Section 7.

**F.S. 794.005**, the entire section: "The Legislature finds that the least
serious sexual battery offense, which is provided in s. 794.011(5), was intended,
and remains intended, to serve as the basic charge of sexual battery and to be
necessarily included in the offenses charged under subsections (3) and (4),
within the meaning of s. 924.34; and that it was never intended that the sexual
battery offense described in s. 794.011(5) require any force or violence beyond
the force and violence that is inherent in the accomplishment of 'penetration' or
'union.'"

**F.S. 794.011(5)**, each of whose four paragraphs contains the phrase "and in
the process does not use physical force and violence likely to cause serious
personal injury."

**F.S. 794.011(6)**, which enacts the lesser-included structure 794.005
describes, paragraph by paragraph: (5)(a) through (5)(c) are included in any
offence charged under (3), and (5)(a), (5)(b), (5)(c), and (5)(d) are each
included in (4)(a), (4)(b), (4)(c), and (4)(d) respectively.

**Why this is unusual and why it is worth a page's differentiator.** Florida's
Legislature legislated **twice** about the same point: once in the operative text
of 794.011(5) and (6), and once, thirteen years after the chapter was written, in
a freestanding findings section that says in terms what the Legislature
"intended" and "remains intended." **No other statute in this project's thirty
pages carries a companion findings section of that kind.** The nearest thing is
F.S. 794.023(1), which is a findings clause inside the same chapter and about a
different question. verified by review of the thirty populated and unpopulated
c9 entries and their statutes.

### 6.4 The cover-the-name test

Cover the words "sexual battery" and read outline section 8.

- Swap in **grand theft** and the sentence is nonsense. F.S. 812.014 escalates on
  the value of the property, from the $750 felony threshold to the $100,000 first
  degree tier. There is no force element in it to be absent, and no findings
  section declaring what the Legislature intended a lesser tier to require.
- Swap in **battery** and the sentence is **false in the exact place it matters**.
  F.S. 784.03(1)(a) requires an intentional touching or striking "against the will
  of the other," and 784.03(1)(b) requires intentionally causing bodily harm.
  Chapter 784 has **no findings section**, no statutory lesser-included scheme of
  the 794.011(6) kind, and no provision anywhere declaring that a tier of the
  offence requires no force beyond what the act itself involves. ST-03 verified
  every operative subsection of chapter 784 and recorded no such provision.
- Swap in **drug trafficking** and it becomes substance-specific weight tiers with
  mandatory minimums. Force is not an element of any tier and never was, so
  "the basic charge is the one with no force in it" is not false, it is
  meaningless.
- Swap in **domestic violence** and it is the wrong axis entirely: F.S. 741.28(3)
  turns on the relationship between the two people, and W2-04's differentiator is
  the pro-prosecution mandate in 741.2901(2). Neither has anything to do with
  what force does to a degree.
- Swap in **DUI** and it is nonsense twice over.
- Swap in **lewd and lascivious battery under F.S. 800.04(4)**, which is the
  hardest and closest swap and is the sibling's statute, and see 6.6.

**The load-bearing clause is "and F.S. 794.005 records the Legislature's
finding."** A findings section that states what a lesser tier of an offence was
never intended to require is true of exactly one statute in this project.
