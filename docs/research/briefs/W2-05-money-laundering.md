## 5. The C9 differentiator, named

**Cover the words "money laundering" and the page is still identifiable,
because Section 3 carries a three-tier threshold ladder that starts at $300 and
is measured across any 12-month period, and no other statute in Florida
aggregates conduct over a rolling year to set the degree.**

The $300 figure alone does most of the C9 work. Readers arrive assuming money
laundering means large sums, and the verified statutory floor is $300. Section 7
passes independently: structuring under F.S. 896.104 is a different offence with
its own ranking-chart rows.

## 6. Sources and verification status

### Verified this session, fetched 2026-08-13

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/896.101** (allowlisted:
flsenate.gov). 2025 Florida Statutes. Statutory title, quoted:

> "Florida Money Laundering Act; definitions; penalties; injunctions; seizure warrants; immunity."

Penalties, subsection (5), quoted in full:

> "(5) A person who violates this section, if the violation involves:
> (a) Financial transactions exceeding $300 but less than $20,000 in any 12-month period, commits a felony of the third degree, punishable as provided in s. 775.082, s. 775.083, or s. 775.084.
> (b) Financial transactions totaling or exceeding $20,000 but less than $100,000 in any 12-month period, commits a felony of the second degree, punishable as provided in s. 775.082, s. 775.083, or s. 775.084.
> (c) Financial transactions totaling or exceeding $100,000 in any 12-month period, commits a felony of the first degree, punishable as provided in s. 775.082, s. 775.083, or s. 775.084."

Subsection (6) additionally provides for penalties beyond those in ss. 775.082,
775.083 and 775.084; the operative text was truncated at the fetch boundary and
is recorded as UNVERIFIED below.

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/921.0022** (allowlisted), offense
severity ranking chart, 2025. Verified rows:

| Level | Subsection | Degree | Description, verbatim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 896.101(5)(a) | 3rd | "Money laundering, financial transactions exceeding $300 but less than $20,000." |
| 8 | 896.101(5)(b) | 2nd | "Money laundering, financial transactions totaling or exceeding $20,000, but less than $100,000." |
| 9 | 896.101(5)(c) | 1st | "Money laundering, financial instruments totaling or exceeding $100,000." |
| 7 | 896.104(4)(a)1. | 3rd | "Structuring transactions to evade reporting or registration requirements" (third degree tier) |
| 8 | 896.104(4)(a)2. | 2nd | "Structuring transactions to evade reporting or registration requirements" (second degree tier) |
| 9 | 896.104(4)(a)3. | 1st | "Structuring transactions to evade reporting or registration requirements" (first degree tier) |

**The finding inside that table, and it is the strongest one on this page.** A
third degree money laundering charge is **offense level 7**. Compare grand
theft, where a third degree charge ranges from level 2 to level 6, and
embezzlement charged as theft at $19,000 is level 4. The same $19,000 charged
under the Florida Money Laundering Act is level 7. **The degree is identical and
the score is not.** That is verified from the chart, it is invisible on every
competitor page in this market, and it is the clearest single demonstration of
why the offense-level column in TEMPLATE-FINDINGS Change 2 is worth building.

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/775.082** and **.../921.0024**
(allowlisted). Maxima and the 44-point and 22-point provisions quoted in
W1-02 Section 6.
