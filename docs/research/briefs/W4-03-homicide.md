## 0. THREE THINGS RESOLVED BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

### 0.1 The offense-level constraint, and it is harder here than on ST-01

**Finding: the most serious charge this page covers sits outside the sentencing
scheme every other state charge page on this site is built around, and the
statute says so in one sentence.**

**F.S. 921.002, 2025 Florida Statutes, verbatim:**

> "The Criminal Punishment Code shall apply to all felony offenses, except
> capital felonies, committed on or after October 1, 1998."

Three verified consequences, each checked separately rather than inferred from
the first:

1. **First degree murder has no row in the F.S. 921.0022 ranking chart.** The
   chart's only 782.04(1) entry is at level 9 and reads "Attempt, conspire, or
   solicit to commit premeditated murder." **verified** by parsing the chart.
   Completed first degree murder is absent.
2. **F.S. 921.0023, which supplies default levels for unlisted felonies, does
   not reach capital felonies either.** Its parameters run third degree, second
   degree, first degree, first degree punishable by life, and life felony.
   **There is no capital entry.** So the ST-01 fallback, "unlisted but
   defaultable," is unavailable here: this offence is not unlisted, it is
   outside the scheme.
3. **F.S. 921.0024's worksheet scores a capital felony only as prior record**,
   under "Prior capital felony points," never as a primary offence.

**Instruction, and it is the same shape as D14's on
`/criminal-defense/computer-crimes-defense` but reached from a different
direction.** The penalties table on this page carries an offense-level column
for the rows that have one and **leaves the capital row's cell empty with a
stated reason in the table itself**, not a dash and not a guess. The stated
reason is a quotation of F.S. 921.002, which is one sentence long and is the
clearest thing on the page.

**Why an empty cell with a reason beats dropping the column.** ST-01 dropped the
column entirely because *no* row on that page had a level. Here, seven rows do:
manslaughter at 7, vehicular homicide at 7, aggravated manslaughter of an
elderly person or disabled adult at 9, aggravated manslaughter of a child at 10,
second degree murder under 782.04(2) at 10, second degree felony murder under
782.04(3) at 9, third degree murder under 782.04(4) at 8. Dropping a column
seven rows can fill in order to accommodate one row that cannot is the wrong
trade, and the one cell that cannot be filled is the most interesting cell on
the page.

**Second instruction, gated.** F.S. 921.0024's victim injury table contains a
category found nowhere else in the worksheet: **"2nd degree murder-death, 240"
points**, against 120 for the general "Death" category. **verified** in the
operative text. This is genuinely charge-specific and genuinely useful. **It is
recorded here and it does not ship as arithmetic.** Computing a lowest
permissible sentence from it is applying the scheme, which ST-01 Section 0
correctly placed on the far side of the C12 allowlist, and the sentencing
calculator has not cleared its ship gate under plan Section 5.6. The page may
quote the worksheet row. It may not do the sum. Routed to the attorney read.

### 0.3 The burden allocation, which is the substance of the recommendation, and it came out the other way

The task framing for this brief anticipated that the burden allocation in the
pretrial immunity procedure would turn out to be case law rather than statute,
in which case it could not ship under a non-attorney byline. **It was checked
and it is statutory.**

**F.S. 776.032(4), 2025 Florida Statutes, verbatim:**

> "In a criminal prosecution, once a prima facie claim of self-defense immunity
> from criminal prosecution has been raised by the defendant at a pretrial
> immunity hearing, the burden of proof by clear and convincing evidence is on
> the party seeking to overcome the immunity from criminal prosecution provided
> in subsection (1)."

Added by s. 1, ch. 2017-72, per the section's own history note. **verified.**

**So the finding is the inverse of the one anticipated: the allocation is inside
the C12 allowlist, it is quotable, and it can ship without an attorney byline.**
Four things are in the text and one is not, and the page must respect the line:

| Proposition | In the statute? | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| There is a pretrial immunity hearing | **yes**, 776.032(4) names it | quotable |
| The defendant raises a prima facie claim first | **yes**, 776.032(4) | quotable |
| The standard is clear and convincing evidence | **yes**, 776.032(4) | quotable |
| The burden falls on the party seeking to overcome immunity | **yes**, 776.032(4) | quotable |
| That party is the State | **not in these words** | **inferred**, one step. State it as the statute states it, or say "the State" only where the sentence makes plain it is a paraphrase |
| What the defendant's prima facie showing requires, and to what quantum | **not in the statute at all** | **do not state it.** No number, no standard, no characterisation. This is the case-law half and it goes under a byline or it comes out |
| How the motion is filed and noticed | **not in 776.032** | **UNMET.** Fla. R. Crim. P. was not fetched for this brief. Do not describe a procedural vehicle from recall |

**The standing rule is satisfied by exclusion, not by silence.** The page says
what 776.032(4) says and stops at the edge of it, and the fact that the statute
stops there is itself worth one sentence, because the top-ranked competitor did
not notice.

**Which produces the sharpest competitive finding in this brief. The
top-ranked Tampa homicide page states the wrong standard.**
`brancatolawfirm.com`, organic position 3 on `homicide attorney Tampa`, carries a
table row reading, verbatim: "Stand Your Ground Immunity | **Preponderance of
evidence at hearing** | Pre-trial immunity hearing." **verified 2026-08-13** by
direct fetch. The operative text of F.S. 776.032(4) says clear and convincing,
and says it of the party seeking to overcome immunity rather than of the
defendant. **That is the pre-2017 allocation, superseded by ch. 2017-72 nine
years ago, published today on the highest-graded homicide page in this market.**

Recorded as a market observation, not as page copy. **Nothing on this site
should name a competitor or characterise its accuracy.** What it establishes is
narrower and more useful: fetching one subsection produces a more accurate page
than the market leader has, which is the entire thesis of the C12 rail.

### 6.2 The four facts inside the assertion, each checked separately

1. **The participant distinction is the pivot.** (1)(a)2 requires the killing be
   "committed by a person engaged in the perpetration"; (3) requires it be "by a
   person other than the person engaged in the perpetration." Same predicate
   felony, opposite actor, capital felony versus first degree felony.
2. **The list lengths differ.** (1)(a)2 runs a. through s., nineteen items. (3)
   runs (a) through (r), eighteen. **verified by enumeration**, not by counting a
   summary.
3. **The missing item is human trafficking**, at (1)(a)2.s. It is absent from
   (3)'s list and absent from the (4) exclusion list. **verified.**
4. **The offense levels differ and the degrees do not.** F.S. 921.0022 ranks
   782.04(2) at **level 10** and 782.04(3) at **level 9**, both first degree
   felonies with the same statutory maximum. **verified** from the parsed chart,
   levels only, per the standing rule.

**F.S. 921.141(2)(b), verbatim:**

> "The jury shall return findings identifying each aggravating factor found to

**F.S. 921.141(2)(c), verbatim, which is the number the task flagged:**

> "If at least eight jurors determine that the defendant should be sentenced to
> death, the jury's recommendation to the court must be a sentence of death. If

**F.S. 921.141(3)(a)2, verbatim, because the eight-juror figure alone is
incomplete and stating it alone would be its own error:**

> "Death, and at least eight jurors recommend a sentence of death, the court,
> after considering each aggravating factor found by the jury and all mitigating
> circumstances, may impose a sentence of life imprisonment without the
> possibility of parole or a sentence of death. The court may consider only an
> aggravating factor that was unanimously found to exist by the jury. The court
> may impose a sentence of death only if the jury unanimously finds at least one
> aggravating factor beyond a reasonable doubt."

**The four things that must travel together on the page**, because separating
them is how the error gets made:

1. Unanimity is required on **the aggravating factor**, and without it the
   defendant is ineligible.
2. **Eight** is the threshold for the jury's **recommendation** of death.
3. A recommendation of life **binds** the court, under (3)(a)1: "the court shall
   impose the recommended sentence of life."
4. A recommendation of death does **not** bind the court, which "may impose" life
   or death.
