Questions 1, 2 and 7 are the FAQ block; all three pass C9 and all three turn on
the state-federal distinction that is this page's reason to exist. Question 4 is
the jurisdictional element in plain language. Question 8 is the multiplication
problem and it is what makes these cases frightening. Question 5 is blocked on
the allowlist and must not be answered with a number until it is resolved.

## 5. The C9 differentiator, named

**Cover the words "wire fraud" and the page is still identifiable, because
Section 2 answers a question that is true of this charge and no other on the
site: the offence the reader has been told they are facing does not exist under
Florida law, and the Florida analogue is a differently named statute with a
different threshold ladder.**

This is also how the overlap pair stays distinct without shared copy, which is
what plan Section 4.6 and C10 require. **The white-collar page's spine is the
Florida Communications Fraud Act and the state-versus-federal question. The
federal page's spine is federal procedure and the guidelines loss table.**
Neither can be reworded into the other, because one is built on a Florida
statute the other never mentions.

**18 U.S.C. 1343, quoted in full:**

> "Whoever, having devised or intending to devise any scheme or artifice to defraud, or for obtaining money or property by means of false or fraudulent pretenses, representations, or promises, transmits or causes to be transmitted by means of wire, radio, or television communication in interstate or foreign commerce, any writings, signs, signals, pictures, or sounds for the purpose of executing such scheme or artifice, shall be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than 20 years, or both. If the violation occurs in relation to, or involving any benefit authorized, transported, transmitted, transferred, disbursed, or paid in connection with, a presidentially declared major disaster or emergency (as those terms are defined in section 102 of the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act (42 U.S.C. 5122)), or affects a financial institution, such person shall be fined not more than $1,000,000 or imprisoned not more than 30 years, or both."

**This unblocks three things.** The statutory maximum is now citable. The
enhancement is now citable, and note that it is triggered by a **presidentially
declared major disaster or emergency** as well as by a financial institution,
which is the provision that connects this page to the PPP brief. And the seeded
FAQ question in src/lib/site.ts, "How much time does a first wire fraud offense
carry?", is no longer blocked and can stay, provided the answer distinguishes
the statutory maximum from the guidelines range rather than presenting the
maximum as an expectation.

**Mail fraud now lives here in part.** Per DECISIONS.md D4,
`/white-collar-defense/mail-fraud` was removed after failing C9 against wire
fraud, and the `mail fraud attorney Tampa` query is targeted by an H2 within
`/federal-criminal-defense/wire-mail-fraud`. **That does not change this page.**
This page is built on F.S. 817.034 and the state-federal question, which is
precisely why it survived the same test. Do not import mail fraud content here.
