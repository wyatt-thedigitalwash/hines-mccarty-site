## 3. The question set a person would ask an AI assistant

1. "I was charged with fraudulent use of personal identification information in
   Florida. What does that mean?"
2. "Is identity theft a felony in Florida?"
3. "How much has to be involved before it becomes a second degree felony?"
4. "What if I used my own relative's information?"
5. "Does it matter that the person was a minor?"
6. "Is identity theft a state charge or a federal charge?"
7. "What if I never actually got any money?"
8. "How many counts can they charge if I used one person's information several
   times?"

Questions 2, 3 and 5 are the FAQ block; all three pass C9. Question 1 is the
direct-answer paragraph. Question 7 is the possession-with-intent point and is
the most useful legal distinction on the page: the statute reaches possession
with intent to use, not only completed use. Question 8 is the multiplication
problem that makes these cases dangerous and that no competitor page addresses.

## 5. The C9 differentiator, named

**Cover the words "identity theft" and the page is still identifiable, because
Section 7 describes two separate, higher-ranked offences that exist only in this
statute: one turning on the age of the person whose information was used, and
one turning on the defendant being that person's parent, legal guardian, or
custodian.**

The second of those, F.S. 817.568(7), is ranked at offense level 9 as a **second
degree** felony with a PBL designation, which is verified and genuinely
striking: it is ranked above a first degree grand theft of $100,000 while
carrying a lower degree. That single fact is nonsensical about any other charge
on the site.

> (b) Any person who willfully and without authorization fraudulently uses personal identification information concerning a person without first obtaining that person's consent commits a fe..."

full before the table publishes its second row.

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/921.0022** (allowlisted), offense
severity ranking chart, 2025. Verified rows, complete for this statute:

| Level | Subsection | Degree | Description, verbatim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 817.568(2)(a) | 3rd | "Fraudulent use of personal identification information." |
| 5 | 817.568(2)(b) | 2nd | "Fraudulent use of personal identification information; value of benefit, services received, payment avoided, or amount of injury or fraud, $5,000 or more or use of personal identifi..." |
| 8 | 817.568(6) | 2nd | "Fraudulent use of personal identification information of an individual under the age of 18." |
| 9 | 817.568(7) | 2nd, PBL | "Fraudulent use of personal identification information of an individual under the age of 18 by his or her parent, legal guardian, or person exercising custodial authority." |

The level 5 description is truncated at the fetch boundary in the same way and
must be captured in full.

**Two findings inside that table.** First, the $5,000 figure in (2)(b) is the
only dollar threshold in this statute's ranking rows, and it is far lower than
the thresholds that move grand theft between degrees, so a modest identity
theft outranks a much larger ordinary theft. Second, the (6) and (7) rows carry
the same degree, second, at two different offense levels, 8 and 9, separated
only by the defendant's relationship to the child. Both are verified and
neither appears on any competitor page in this SERP.

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/775.082** and **.../921.0024**
(allowlisted). Maxima and the 44-point and 22-point provisions quoted in W1-02
Section 6.

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1028A**
(allowlisted 2026-08-13 per DECISIONS.md D2). Current 2024 Main Ed. (1/6/2025).
**18 U.S.C. 1028A(a)(1), quoted:**

> "Whoever, during and in relation to any felony violation enumerated in subsection (c), knowingly transfers, possesses, or uses, without lawful authority, a means of identification of another person shall, in addition to the punishment provided for such felony, be sentenced to a term of imprisonment of 2 years."

Subsection (b), verified: probation is not available; the two-year term runs
**consecutively** to the sentence for the underlying felony; the court may not
reduce the underlying felony sentence to offset it; and concurrent running is
permitted only against other simultaneous 1028A counts, in accordance with
Sentencing Commission guidelines.

**This is the forum section's whole argument, and it is now citable.** A Florida
identity theft charge is scored on a scoresheet where the level does most of the
work. A federal 1028A count is a flat two years bolted on top of whatever the
underlying felony draws, with no probation and no offset. Those are not two
versions of the same exposure, and the structure of 1028A is also why
DECISIONS.md D5 declines to build a standalone federal page: the statute attaches
to an enumerated felony rather than standing alone.
