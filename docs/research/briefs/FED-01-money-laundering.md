## 0. THE CONSTRAINT, AND THE VERDICT

**The constraint.** 18 U.S.C. 1956(a)(1) and F.S. 896.101(3)(a) are near word
for word identical. The federal page must therefore be built on **the predicate
list, the absence of thresholds, and federal sentencing**, and **may not restate
the conduct language**. If only the conduct language supports a page, the pair
collapses.

**Verdict: CONSTRAINT SATISFIED. The pair does not collapse.** The page has more
distinct substance than the constraint required, and the SERP is actively asking
for exactly the material the constraint points at.

| Constraint limb | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Predicate list | **Satisfied, verified** | 1956(c)(7) incorporates the RICO list at 1961(1) plus six further categories. "List of specified unlawful activity money laundering" is a **live related search** on the informational query. |
| Absence of thresholds | **Satisfied, verified, and better than expected** | 1956 sets no dollar threshold at all. Its companion, 1957, turns on a single $10,000 figure. So the federal scheme is threshold-free and threshold-gated in two statutes, against Florida's three-tier ladder. That is a three-way contrast, not an absence. |
| Federal sentencing | **Satisfied, verified** | USSC FY2025: 1,170 money laundering offenders, 90 percent sentenced to prison, average 69 months. |
| Not the conduct language | **Satisfiable** | None of the above requires restating 1956(a)(1). The outline in Section 4 puts the conduct language in one short subordinate passage that exists only to say "this part is the same, here is what is not." |

**One limb is partially unmet.** USSG 2S1.1's base-offense-level mechanics are
UNVERIFIED after two fetch attempts. See Section 6. The sentencing limb stands
on the USSC statistics, which are verified and current.

## 6. Sources and verification status

### Verified this session, fetched 2026-08-13

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1956**
(allowlisted per D2). Current 2024 Main Ed. (1/6/2025).

Penalty for 1956(a)(1), quoted:
> "a fine of not more than $500,000 or twice the value of the property involved in the transaction, whichever is greater, or imprisonment for not more than twenty years, or both."

Specified unlawful activity, 1956(c)(7), categories verified:
> (A) acts listed in the RICO statute at 18 U.S.C. 1961(1), excluding certain currency reporting violations; (B) specified foreign offences, including drug manufacturing and distribution, violent crimes, fraud against foreign banks, public corruption, munitions smuggling, extraditable offences, and human trafficking; (C) continuing criminal enterprises under the Controlled Substances Act; (D) numerous federal crimes spanning aircraft destruction, espionage, counterfeiting, smuggling, bank fraud, terrorism-related offences, and environmental violations; (E) felony violations of water pollution, ocean dumping, ship pollution, safe drinking water, and waste disposal statutes; (F) federal health care fraud offences; (G) endangered species trafficking exceeding $10,000 in value.

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1957**
(allowlisted). Current 2024 Main Ed.
> (a) threshold: "criminally derived property of a value greater than $10,000"
> (f)(1) monetary transaction: "the deposit, withdrawal, transfer, or exchange, in or affecting interstate or foreign commerce, of funds or a monetary instrument ... by, through, or to a financial institution"
> (b)(1) penalty: "a fine under title 18, United States Code, or imprisonment for not more than ten years or both"

**https://www.ussc.gov/research/quick-facts/money-laundering** (allowlisted).
Fiscal year 2025 data.
> "1,170 involved money laundering" of 66,662 total cases reported to the Commission
> "90% were sentenced to prison."
> "The average sentence length for individuals sentenced for money laundering offenses was 69 months."
