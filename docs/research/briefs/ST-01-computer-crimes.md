## 0. THE CONSTRAINT, AND THE VERDICT

**The constraint, from D14.** This page cannot carry an offense-level column.
F.S. 815.06 has no row in the F.S. 921.0022 offense severity ranking chart, and
815.04's rows must not be borrowed for it. Almost every other state charge page
in this set anchors its C9 on that table. This one cannot, so the anchor has to
be named explicitly and justified rather than assumed.

**Verdict: the constraint is satisfied, and the replacement anchor is stronger
than the column it replaces.**

**The anchor is F.S. 815.06(3), the escalation ladder, which is keyed to the
kind of harm rather than to an amount.** Verified and quoted in full in Section
6. Its shape:

| Subsection | What escalates it | Degree | Statutory maximum, F.S. 775.082 |
|---|---|---|---|
| (3)(a) | nothing, this is the base offense on a violation of (2) | third degree | 5 years |
| (3)(b)1 | damage or loss of at least $5,000 | second degree | 15 years |
| (3)(b)2 | a purpose of devising or executing a scheme to defraud or obtain property | second degree | 15 years |
| (3)(b)3 | interrupting or impairing a governmental operation, or public communication, transportation, water, gas, or other public service | second degree | 15 years |
| (3)(b)4 | intentionally interrupting data to or from, or gaining unauthorized access to, a computer or device belonging to any mode of public or private transit under s. 341.031 | second degree | 15 years |
| (3)(c)1 | endangering human life | first degree | 30 years |
| (3)(c)2 | disrupting a computer, system, network, or electronic device that affects medical equipment used in the direct administration of medical care or treatment to a person | first degree | 30 years |
| (4) | willfully, knowingly, and without authorization modifying equipment or supplies used or intended to be used in a computer or device | first degree misdemeanor | not a felony |

**Why this is a legitimate anchor and not a consolation prize.** Every other
ladder this project has verified in the Florida statutes is a money ladder:
grand theft by property value, money laundering at $300, $20,000, and $100,000
across a 12-month window. F.S. 815.06 is the one where the dollar figure is a
single entry on a four-item list at the second degree tier and disappears
entirely at the first degree tier, which is reached by endangering a person or
by disrupting medical equipment in direct patient care. That is a
harm-consequence ladder, not a value ladder, and it is charge-specific in a way
no offense-level column would have been.

**A second, separate fact was found while testing the constraint, and it is
gated rather than shipped.** F.S. 921.0023 supplies default severity levels for
felonies the ranking chart does not list. Verified and quoted in Section 6.
Because 815.06 is unlisted, the default parameters would apply to it.

**Instruction: do not put those defaults in a column, and do not present them
alongside the ranked levels other charge pages carry.** Three reasons, in order
of weight.

1. D14's constraint is a constraint, and a defaults column is an offense-level
   column wearing a different label. A reader comparing this page to the grand
   theft page would read one number against the other and conclude the
   Legislature ranked both. It did not rank this one.
2. Applying 921.0023 to a specific unlisted statute is a legal conclusion, not a
   quotation. It sits on the far side of the C12 verification allowlist.
3. The page does not need it. The anchor above does the work.

Recorded in Section 6 as verified-but-gated, with the decision routed to the
attorney read rather than resolved here.

## 5. The C9 differentiator

Ready to populate the `c9` field in src/lib/site.ts:

```
statute:   "F.S. 815.06"
section:   "What turns a third degree charge into a second or first degree charge"
assertion: "Florida escalates unauthorized computer access by the kind of harm rather than by an amount: the base offense is a third degree felony on the access itself, the second degree attaches on $5,000 in damage or loss, a fraud purpose, disruption of a government or public service, or a transit system, and the first degree is reached only by endangering human life or disrupting medical equipment used in the direct administration of care."
```

### Why it is false or nonsensical if another charge is swapped in

Cover the words "computer" and "unauthorized access" and read section 5. No
other offense in this project's set reaches a first degree felony by way of
medical equipment used in direct patient care, and none escalates on transit
systems by reference to s. 341.031. Swap grand theft into the heading and the
first degree tier becomes $100,000 of property, not a ventilator. Swap money
laundering in and the ladder becomes $300, $20,000, and $100,000 measured across
a rolling 12 months. Swap drug trafficking in and it becomes weight. **Every
other Florida ladder verified in this project counts something. This one asks
what the access put at risk.** That sentence is true of exactly one page.

### Pairwise check against FED-02, performed by hand

`statuteKey` resolves this to `fs 815.06` and the sibling to `18 usc 1030`.
Different statutes, so **the automated checker will never compare them.** FED-02
Section 6 anticipated exactly this and left a standing instruction for it. This
is the check.

| | ST-01, this page | FED-02, the sibling |
|---|---|---|
| Statute | F.S. 815.06 | 18 U.S.C. 1030 |
| Structural claim | **one offense with a consequence ladder above it** | **three separate offenses, each with its own elements** |
| What escalates | the kind of harm: fraud purpose, public service, transit, human life, medical equipment | nothing escalates; (a)(1), (a)(4), (a)(5) are distinct offenses |
| Role of the $5,000 figure | one of four routes to the second degree | a carve-out that keeps conduct out of (a)(4) |
| Role of damage | one of four routes to the second degree, gated at $5,000 | the whole of (a)(5), with **no dollar floor** |
| Top of the ladder | medical equipment and human life | no analogue |
| National defense information | no analogue in Florida | (a)(1), FED-02's cleanest limb |

**No collision.** The two assertions are not restatements and they are not
near-neighbours. FED-02's rests on the federal statute containing three
offenses; this one rests on the Florida statute containing one offense and a
non-monetary escalation ladder. The one place they touch, damage at $5,000, they
touch in opposite directions, and FED-02 already uses that contrast in its own
favour, which means both pages are strengthened by it rather than duplicated.

**Checked by hand against the other populated assertions as well.** None rests
on F.S. 815.06 or F.S. 815.04. W2-05 money laundering rests on a value ladder
across a 12-month window and is the closest structural neighbour, and it is the
one this assertion is explicitly written against.
