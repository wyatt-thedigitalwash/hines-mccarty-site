### Verified this session

**https://www.flmd.uscourts.gov/divisions/** (allowlisted). Quoted text is

**Added 2026-08-13 after the DECISIONS.md D2 allowlist amendment.** Both fetched
from uscode.house.gov, Current 2024 Main Ed. (1/6/2025).

**18 U.S.C. 1343 (wire fraud)**, quoted in full in W2-07 Section 6. The clause
that matters most to this page:
> "If the violation occurs in relation to, or involving any benefit authorized, transported, transmitted, transferred, disbursed, or paid in connection with, a presidentially declared major disaster or emergency (as those terms are defined in section 102 of the Robert T. Stafford Disaster Relief and Emergency Assistance Act (42 U.S.C. 5122)), or affects a financial institution, such person shall be fined not more than $1,000,000 or imprisoned not more than 30 years, or both."

**18 U.S.C. 1344 (bank fraud)**, quoted:
> whoever "knowingly executes, or attempts to execute, a scheme or artifice" either "to defraud a financial institution" or "to obtain any of the moneys, funds, credits, assets, securities, or other property owned by, or under the custody or control of, a financial institution, by means of false or fraudulent pretenses, representations, or promises"

with a maximum fine of $1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 30 years.

**This changes the shape of the page and should be treated as a finding, not
just a citation.** The wire fraud enhancement is triggered by a **presidentially
declared major disaster or emergency**, and PPP was a pandemic relief programme.
That is a statutory hook connecting this charge to a 30-year maximum on the face
of the statute, without needing the limitations argument at all. It is also
charge-specific in the C9 sense: no other page in the white-collar silo turns on
a disaster declaration. **Verify how that clause is actually applied before
building a section on it**, since the text establishes the clause exists, not
that it reaches every PPP case.
