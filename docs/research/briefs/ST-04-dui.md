source material only, no drafted page copy.

### 0.4 What this changes, and what it does not

**D6 is not wrong as a general rule. It is wrong as a universal one.** Its
reasoning was that measuring a page against a term with no measurable volume
produces a number that means nothing. That reasoning is sound and it still
governs grand theft, drug trafficking, gun crimes, money laundering, identity
theft, wire fraud, PPP loan fraud, battery, and drug possession. It does not
govern DUI, because the premise it rests on is factually absent here.

**Recommended amendment, stated for DECISIONS.md rather than made here:**

> D6 is amended to read: wave 1 through wave 3 pages are measured on statewide
> informational capture and on conversion, **except where a charge term carries
> measurable Tampa DMA volume, in which case local position is a legitimate
> secondary measure and is reported alongside the informational measure.** As of
> 2026-08-13 exactly two charges qualify: DUI (320/month) and domestic violence
> (90/month). Every other charge term in waves 1 through 3 remains unmeasurable
> and is reported under the original standard. The list is re-derived from a
> volume pull at each reporting cycle rather than assumed.

**What must not follow from this.** The contradiction is about measurability, not
about winnability. Those are different questions and Section 1 answers the second
one unfavourably. **`dui lawyer tampa` is now measurable and is still graded
YEARS.** A page can be honestly reported on a term it is not expected to win, and
that is in fact the correct handling here: report the position, report that it is
not the target, and report the informational terms that are.

**Client communication requirement carries over from D6.** D6 required that its
reporting standard be communicated before reporting begins. The amendment has the
same requirement and the same reason. A standard that acquires an exception
partway through reads as a moving goalpost if it is introduced with the numbers.

## 2. THE FINDING: refusal became a crime on the first refusal, and nobody has updated

### 2.1 What the statute now says

**F.S. 316.1939(1)(e), 2025 Florida Statutes, fetched from flsenate.gov
2026-08-13, quoted verbatim:**

> "Who, after having been so informed, refused to submit to any such test when
> requested to do so by a law enforcement officer or correctional officer commits
> a misdemeanor of the second degree for a first refusal or a misdemeanor of the
> first degree for a second or subsequent refusal, and is subject to punishment
> as provided in s. 775.082 or s. 775.083."

### 2.2 What it said before, verified by fetching the prior editions

The same subparagraph, **2023 and 2024 Florida Statutes**, both fetched from
flsenate.gov 2026-08-13, identical to each other, quoted verbatim:

> "Who, after having been so informed, refused to submit to any such test when
> requested to do so by a law enforcement officer or correctional officer commits
> a misdemeanor of the first degree and is subject to punishment as provided in
> s. 775.082 or s. 775.083."

And the predicate at (d) in those editions **required a prior**, verbatim:

> "Who was informed that a refusal to submit to a lawful test of his or her
> breath or urine, **if his or her driving privilege has been previously
> suspended or if he or she has previously been fined under s. 327.35215 for a
> prior refusal**..."

**So the pre-2025 structure was: a first refusal was not a crime under this
section at all, and a second or subsequent refusal was a first degree
misdemeanor.** That is the version every source in Section 2.4 is still
publishing. It was correct until October 2025.

### 2.3 The effective date, verified from the Legislature

**F.S. 316.1939 history line, 2025 edition, verbatim:**

> "History. -- s. 5, ch. 2002-263; s. 2, ch. 2021-184; **s. 4, ch. 2025-121**."

**Effective date, from the Florida Legislature's own 2025 Regular Session
Chapter Number / Effective Date Report**, fetched from leg.state.fl.us
2026-08-13:

| Chapter | Bill | Type | Effective |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-120 | H 00383 | General Bill/CS | 07/01/2025 |
| **2025-121** | **H 00687** | **General Bill/CS** | **10/01/2025** |
| 2025-122 | H 00703 | General Bill/CS | 10/01/2025 |

**Chapter 2025-121, CS/HB 687, effective October 1, 2025. verified.**

**The date is not decoration, it is an element of the page's accuracy.** A
refusal on 30 September 2025 is governed by the old text and a refusal on 1
October 2025 is not. The page must say the date, because a reader whose case
predates it will otherwise read the wrong law about their own case. This is the
single most important reason the page carries a last-reviewed date under template
item 10.

### 2.5 Why this is the recommendation and what its limits are

**This is the same pattern TOOLS.md item 2 produced for the 33-day rule, with the
direction reversed and the stakes higher.** There, a widely repeated figure was
wrong and the correction page was the asset. Here, a widely repeated figure was
**right until ten months ago** and is now wrong, and the correction is the asset.
The difference matters: the 33-day corpus was wrong through carelessness, this
one is wrong through staleness, and staleness is the dimension
content-gaps.md and TEMPLATE-FINDINGS Section 4 both identify as the most
beatable in this market.

`refusing breathalyzer florida` carries **210/month at Florida level and 50/month
at Tampa DMA**, competition LOW, CPC $2.30. It is not the biggest number on the
page. It is the number attached to the only question on which this firm can be
straightforwardly more accurate than the state agency, three DUI-only firms, and
Google's AI Overview simultaneously.

**Three limits, stated so the page does not overreach.**

1. **316.1939 reaches breath and urine. It does not reach blood the same way.**
   The operative text at (1) is "refused to submit to a chemical or physical test
   of his or her **breath or urine**, as described in s. 316.1932." Blood appears
   in the prior-refusal predicate at (d) and in the license-suspension provisions,
   but the offense limb names breath and urine. **Do not write that refusing a
   blood test is a second degree misdemeanor under this section.**
2. **The offense has five conjunctive predicates, (a) through (e).** Probable
   cause, lawful arrest for a 316.193 violation, the suspension warning, the
   criminal warning, and then the refusal. **The warning at (d) is an element**,
   which means the page must not describe refusal as automatically criminal.
   Quote the structure.
3. **This brief verified the statute. It did not verify charging practice.**
   Whether the State Attorney for the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit is filing first
   refusals as a separate count is **UNVERIFIED** and must not be asserted or
   implied. See Section 7.

## 5. What the statutes actually say

All fetched 2026-08-13 from flsenate.gov, 2025 Florida Statutes, which is
allowlisted under plan Section 10.3. Full quotations and URLs are in Section 7.
This section is the shape of the page's substance.

### 5.1 The offense, F.S. 316.193(1)

Three limbs, verbatim: under the influence of alcoholic beverages, a s. 877.111
chemical substance, or a chapter 893 controlled substance **"when affected to the
extent that the person's normal faculties are impaired"**; **or** blood-alcohol
of 0.08 or more grams per 100 milliliters of blood; **or** breath-alcohol of 0.08
or more grams per 210 liters of breath.

**The first limb has no number in it at all.** A prosecution can proceed on
impaired normal faculties with no test result. Every competitor page graded leads
with 0.08. Leading with the disjunction is both more accurate and more useful.

### 5.2 The penalty ladder, with offense levels

Degrees and maxima from F.S. 316.193. Offense levels from the F.S. 921.0022
offense severity ranking chart, parsed 2026-08-13. Per TEMPLATE-FINDINGS Change
2 this table is not optional.

| Subsection | What triggers it | Degree | Maximum | Offense level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 316.193(2)(a) | first conviction | **statute assigns no degree** | 6 months, fine $500 to $1,000 | none |
| 316.193(2)(a) | second conviction | **statute assigns no degree** | 9 months, fine $1,000 to $2,000 | none |
| 316.193(2)(b)1 | third violation, offense occurring **within 10 years after a prior conviction** | third degree felony | 5 years | **3** |
| 316.193(2)(b)2 | third violation, **more than** 10 years after a prior conviction | **not a felony** | 12 months, fine $2,000 to $5,000 | none |
| 316.193(2)(b)3 | **fourth or subsequent violation, regardless of when priors occurred** | third degree felony | 5 years | **6** |
| 316.193(3)(c)1 | causes damage to property or person of another | first degree misdemeanor | -- | none |
| 316.193(3)(c)2 | causes serious bodily injury as defined in s. 316.1933 | third degree felony | 5 years | **7** |
| 316.193(3)(c)3.a | causes death of a human being or unborn child, DUI manslaughter | second degree felony | 15 years | **8** |
| 316.193(3)(c)3.b | DUI manslaughter, knew or should have known of the crash and failed to give information and render aid under s. 316.062 | first degree felony | 30 years | **9** |
| 316.193(3)(c)3.c | DUI manslaughter with a prior conviction under 3.c, s. 327.35(3)(a)3.c., s. 782.071, or s. 782.072 | first degree felony | 30 years | **9** |

**Plus, for any DUI manslaughter conviction, verbatim: "A person who is convicted
of DUI manslaughter shall be sentenced to a mandatory minimum term of
imprisonment of 4 years."**

**Three facts in this table that no page in the field carries.**

**Fact A. Two rows are the same subsection and the same degree and differ by
three offense levels.** 316.193(2)(b) at a third conviction is **level 3**. The
same paragraph at a fourth or subsequent conviction is **level 6**. Both are
third degree felonies with the same 5-year statutory maximum. **The statutory
maximum does not move and the scoresheet doubles.** This is the sharpest single
fact available for this page and it is invisible to anyone reading only 316.193,
which is every page graded in Section 3.

**Fact B. The third-DUI felony is not "the third one within 10 years of the
second."** The text is "a third violation of this section for an offense that
occurs within 10 years after **a prior conviction**." flhsmv.gov's own page
paraphrases this as "Third offense within 10 years of the second conviction,"
which is narrower than the statute. **Quote the statute.**

**Fact C. F.S. 316.193(2)(a) never assigns a misdemeanor degree.** Verified by
absence in the fetched text: (2)(a) prescribes a fine and a term of imprisonment
directly and does not carry the "punishable as provided in s. 775.082 or s.
775.083" formula that (2)(b)1 and (3)(c)1 both carry. **Do not write "second
degree misdemeanor" for a first DUI.** The 6-month maximum matches neither
misdemeanor degree in s. 775.082. Scoped claim: verified as absent from the text
fetched, not a claim that no other statute assigns one.

### 5.3 The 0.15 and minor-passenger enhancement, F.S. 316.193(4)

Triggered by blood-alcohol or breath-alcohol of **0.15 or higher**, **or** by
being "accompanied in the vehicle by a person under the age of 18 years."

| | First conviction | Second conviction | Third or subsequent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fine | $1,000 to $2,000 | $2,000 to $4,000 | not less than $4,000 |
| Imprisonment | not more than 9 months | not more than 12 months | -- |

**The qualifier that matters, verbatim:** "For the purposes of this subsection,
**only the instant offense is required to be a violation of subsection (1) by a
person who has a blood-alcohol level or breath-alcohol level of 0.15 or higher.**"
The current offense carries the enhanced range even where the priors were not
0.15 cases.

**And the ignition interlock split, which is a "may" against a "shall" on the
same facts.** F.S. 316.193(2)(c): where the person had 0.08 or higher, the court
**may** order an interlock for at least 6 continuous months on a first offense.
F.S. 316.193(4)(c): where the person had 0.15 or higher or a minor passenger, the
court **shall** order not less than 6 continuous months for a first offense and
not less than 2 continuous years for a second. Same device, same period,
discretionary in one paragraph and mandatory in the next.

### 5.4 What the court must order on any conviction, F.S. 316.193(5) and (6)

Verified: monthly reporting probation and a substance abuse course by a licensed
DUI program including a psychosocial evaluation, under (5). Under (6)(a), on a
first conviction, probation not to exceed 1 year, **a minimum of 50 hours** of
public service or community work, and impoundment or immobilization for **10
days**, with the total of probation and incarceration capped at 1 year. Under
(6)(b), second conviction within 5 years of a prior conviction: **not less than
10 days imprisonment**, of which **at least 48 hours must be consecutive**, plus
**30 days** impoundment of all vehicles owned by the defendant. Under (6)(c),
third or subsequent within 10 years of a prior conviction: **not less than 30
days**, at least 48 hours consecutive, plus **90 days** impoundment.

**Note the impoundment scope changes between (6)(a) and (6)(b).** First
conviction: the vehicle operated, **or any one vehicle** registered in the
defendant's name. Second: **all vehicles owned by the defendant.** No page graded
in Section 3 distinguishes them.

### 5.5 Implied consent, F.S. 316.1932

The warning the officer must give is quoted in full in Section 7. Its two halves,
for breath and separately for urine, are the suspension consequence and the
criminal consequence, and **both halves are elements of the 316.1939 offense**
under (1)(c) and (1)(d).

Suspension consequence, verbatim: **"1 year for a first refusal, or for a period
of 18 months if the driving privilege of such person has been previously
suspended or if he or she has previously been fined under s. 327.35215 as a
result of a refusal."**

Criminal consequence, verbatim: **"he or she commits a misdemeanor of the second
degree ... or a misdemeanor of the first degree ... if his or her driving
privilege has been previously suspended or if he or she has previously been fined
under s. 327.35215 for a prior refusal."**

**And, verbatim, on both the breath and urine limbs: "The refusal to submit to a
chemical or physical breath test upon the request of a law enforcement officer as
provided in this section is admissible into evidence in any criminal
proceeding."** Refusing does not remove evidence from the case. It substitutes
different evidence, and adds a charge.

**The 18-month trigger is broader than "a second refusal."** It reaches a person
previously **fined under s. 327.35215**, which is the boating refusal provision.
A prior on the water counts. Recorded because "second refusal" is the shorthand
everyone uses and it is not what the statute says.

### 5.6 Blood, F.S. 316.1933

This is not a consent statute. Verbatim, (1)(a): where an officer has probable
cause to believe a vehicle driven by a person under the influence **"has caused
the death or serious bodily injury of a human being,"** the officer **"shall
require"** a blood test, **"may use reasonable force if necessary,"** and,
verbatim, **"Notwithstanding s. 316.1932, the testing required by this paragraph
need not be incidental to a lawful arrest of the person."**

**And it supplies the definition the felony tier turns on**, at (1)(b), verbatim:

> "The term 'serious bodily injury' means an injury to any person, **including
> the driver**, which consists of a physical condition that creates a substantial
> risk of death, serious personal disfigurement, or protracted loss or impairment
> of the function of any bodily member or organ."

**"Including the driver" is the sleeper in this statute.** The third degree
felony at 316.193(3)(c)2 is defined by reference to a definition that expressly
counts an injury to the driver. Not one page graded in Section 3 mentions it.

Also verified at (2)(a)1: a health care provider treating a crash patient who
becomes aware from a blood test done for medical purposes that the patient's
blood-alcohol meets the 316.193(1)(b) level **may notify law enforcement**, and
that notice is limited by (2)(a)2 to the patient's name, who drew the blood, the
level, and the date and time. This is a real and widely unknown route by which a
DUI case begins, and it belongs on the page.

### 5.7 The administrative case, F.S. 322.2615 and 322.271(7)

Section 6 is built on this. The full quotations are in Section 7. The structure:

- **(1)(a).** The officer, on behalf of the department, suspends the driving
  privilege, **takes the license**, and **"issue[s] the person a 10-day temporary
  permit if the person is otherwise eligible for the driving privilege."**
- **(1)(b)2.** "The suspension period **shall commence on the date of issuance of
  the notice of suspension.**" Not on conviction. Not on a court date.
- **(1)(b)3.** The 10-day window and its three options. Quoted in Section 6.1.
- **(1)(b)4.** The temporary permit **"expires at midnight of the 10th day
  following the date of issuance of the notice of suspension."**
- **(3).** Where the department, rather than the officer, issues the notice, the
  temporary permit **"expires 10 days after the date of issuance"** and **is not
  issued at all** where the notice is mailed under s. 322.251.
- **(9).** "A request for a formal review hearing or an informal review hearing
  **shall not stay the suspension** of the person's driver license." And: if the
  department fails to schedule the formal review within 30 days of the request,
  **"the department shall invalidate the suspension."**
- **(10)(a) and (b).** The hard-suspension periods before business-purposes
  eligibility, if the suspension is sustained: **90 days** for a refusal, **30
  days** for a 0.08 or higher.
- **(7).** The scope of the review is **limited by statute to two issues** (three
  on a refusal), and probable cause is one of them.
- **(11).** "If the arresting officer or the breath technician fails to appear
  pursuant to a subpoena as provided in subsection (6), **the department shall
  invalidate the suspension.**"

## 6. The C9 differentiator

Ready to populate the `c9` field in `src/lib/site.ts`:

```
statute:   "F.S. 322.2615"
section:   "The other case: the 10 days that run from the notice, not from a court date"
assertion: "A Florida DUI arrest opens a second case against the driver license that runs on its own clock, and F.S. 322.2615(1)(b)3 gives 10 days from the date the notice of suspension is issued, not from the arrest and not from any court date, to do one of three mutually exclusive things: request a formal review, request an informal review, or take the restricted business-purposes privilege under F.S. 322.271(7), which by that statute's own terms is a waiver of both reviews and is open only to a person who has never had a license suspended under 322.2615, never been disqualified under 322.64, and never been convicted under 316.193."
```

### 6.1 The operative text, quoted, because the number had to be checked

**This project has been burned once by a widely repeated number.** TOOLS.md item
2 records the 33-day rule resolving to 30 days, with day 33 being the earliest
automatic release date and not the deadline at all. **The "10 day" figure in the
PAA question was therefore treated as a claim to be refuted, not a fact to be
built on.**

**F.S. 322.2615(1)(b)3, 2025 Florida Statutes, verbatim:**

> "The driver may request a formal or informal review of the suspension by the
> department within 10 days after the date of issuance of the notice of
> suspension or may request a review of eligibility for a restricted driving
> privilege under s. 322.271(7)."

**The number survives. The trigger does not.** Three findings:

**Finding 1. Ten days is correct.** Unlike the 33-day figure, this one is in the
operative text, twice: the review window at (1)(b)3 and the permit expiry at
(1)(b)4. It is corroborated by a third provision at (3). **The PAA question's
number is right.**

**Finding 2. The trigger is the date of issuance of the notice of suspension, not
the date of arrest, and the difference is not academic.** The task framing for
this brief stated the window "runs from the arrest date." **That is the common
paraphrase and the statute does not say it.** flhsmv.gov makes the same
substitution, verbatim: "The officer will issue the driver a temporary permit
valid for 10 days **from the date of arrest**."

In the ordinary roadside case the two coincide, because the officer issues the
notice at the scene or at booking under (1)(a). **They come apart in exactly the
case where it matters most.** Under (1)(a), where a blood test was administered
the results are transmitted to the department **"within 5 days after receipt of
the results"**, and only then, verbatim, **"the department shall suspend the
person's driver license pursuant to subsection (3)."** Subsection (3) has the
department issue the notice of suspension and a temporary permit **"that expires
10 days after the date of issuance."** In a blood case the notice can issue weeks
after the arrest, and a driver counting 10 days from the arrest date reaches the
wrong conclusion twice: first that the deadline has long passed, and later that
they have no permit when subsection (3) says they may.

**And subsection (3) carries a second trap:** the temporary permit issues
**"unless the notice is mailed pursuant to s. 322.251."** A mailed notice starts
the clock without a permit in the envelope.

**This is the page's correction, and it is a better one than the 33-day
correction was**, because the popular version is not merely imprecise, it is
wrong in a specific, identifiable class of cases, and it is wrong in the same
direction on the state agency's own page.

**Finding 3. The statute does not call it a "demand."** It is a **request** for a
**formal review** or an **informal review**, and the two are different
proceedings: (4) makes the informal review a paper examination with **"the
presence of an officer or witness is not required"**, while (6)(b) gives the
formal review a hearing officer who may administer oaths, take testimony, and
**issue subpoenas**. The choice between them is a real one, and (11) attaches the
whole leverage of the process to it: **subpoena the arresting officer or the
breath technician, and if they fail to appear, "the department shall invalidate
the suspension."** That option does not exist on the informal track.

### 6.2 The third option, and why it is the sharp end

**F.S. 322.271(7), 2025 Florida Statutes, verbatim:**

> "Notwithstanding the provisions of s. 322.2615(10)(a) and (b), a person who has
> never previously had a driver license suspended under s. 322.2615, has never
> been disqualified under s. 322.64, has never been convicted of a violation of
> s. 316.193, and whose driving privilege is now suspended under s. 322.2615 is
> eligible for a restricted driving privilege pursuant to a hearing under
> subsection (2)."

**And (7)(c), verbatim:**

> "Acceptance of the reinstated driving privilege as provided in this subsection
> is deemed a waiver of the right to formal and informal review under s.
> 322.2615. The waiver may not be used as evidence in any other proceeding."

**The fork, stated as the page states it.** Within 10 days the driver either
contests the suspension, and if it is sustained cannot drive at all for 30 days
after the permit expires on a 0.08 case or **90 days** on a refusal under
322.2615(10)(a) and (b), **or** takes an immediate business-purposes-only
privilege and gives up the right to contest it. **A trade, on a 10-day clock,
made before anything is known about the criminal case.**

And it is available to a narrower group than the field says. Three "never"
conditions, conjunctive. Section 3.3 shows the strongest competitor page in the
market states the trade without stating any of them.

### 6.3 The cover-the-name test

Cover the word "DUI" and cover "driving," and read the section.

No other charge on this site opens a **second, separate case against a
government-issued license, administered by a different agency, on a 10-day clock
that starts before charges are filed.** Swap grand theft into the heading and
nothing in the section is true: there is no notice of suspension, no temporary
permit, no formal review, and no waiver. Swap domestic violence in and the same.
Swap drug trafficking in and the same. **The nearest structural neighbour on the
whole site is W2-04 domestic violence, and it is not close:** that page's anchor
is a statutory direction to prosecutors about how a criminal case is handled,
inside the criminal case. This one is a proceeding that is not the criminal case
at all, and 322.2615(14)(a) says so in terms: the department's decision **"may
not be considered in any trial for a violation of s. 316.193."**

**And the assertion has a second cover-test property that is worth stating,
because it is unusual.** Most C9 assertions on this site are true of a statute.
This one is true of a **timeline**. Cover the statute numbers and the sentence is
still nonsensical for every other charge, because no other charge has a deadline
that runs before the first court date.
