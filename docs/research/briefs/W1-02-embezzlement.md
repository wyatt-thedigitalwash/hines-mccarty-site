## 5. The C9 differentiator, named

**REVISED 2026-08-13 after a cross-brief C9 collision.** The first draft named
the F.S. 812.014 value-to-offense-level table as this page's C9 substance. So
did the grand theft brief. Both passed the cover-the-name test individually and
would have produced two pages built on one table. The collision and its ruling
are recorded in CASES-WE-DEFEND-PLAN.md Section 4.5.

**The ruling.** The table lives once, in the Layer 2 offense-level comparison
instrument (TOOLS.md item 1), and this page links to it rather than reproducing
it. Grand theft keeps the value ladder, because the ladder is what that charge
is. This page keeps the better half of its original claim.

**The C9, as revised. Cover the word "embezzlement" and the page is still
identifiable, because Section 2 explains that no Florida offense exists under
that name and the conduct is prosecuted as theft under F.S. 812.014, and because
the page turns throughout on a fact pattern in which possession was lawful and
authorised at the moment it began.**

That second half is the stronger differentiator and it was underweighted in the
first draft. Every other theft-family page on this site describes a taking. This
page describes a person who was **given** the property, lawfully, and is accused
of what happened next. That is why the contested element here is intent to
deprive rather than the taking, why the restitution question arrives on day one,
why the reader is usually still employed or newly fired rather than arrested,
and why the aggregation-over-time problem exists at all. None of that is true of
grand theft, and none of it survives swapping in another charge.

**What this changes in the outline.** Section 3's table becomes a short
degree-and-maximum table with a link out to the Layer 2 comparison instrument
for the offense levels, rather than the full ladder. Sections 4, 5 and the
restitution section carry more weight. The page gets shorter and more distinct
at the same time, which is the right trade.

## 6. Sources and verification status

### Verified this session, fetched 2026-08-13

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/812.014** (allowlisted:
flsenate.gov). 2025 Florida Statutes.

Elements, F.S. 812.014(1), quoted:
> "A person commits theft if he or she knowingly obtains or uses, or endeavors to obtain or to use, the property of another with intent to, either temporarily or permanently: (a) Deprive the other person of a right to the property or a benefit from the property. (b) Appropriate the property to his or her own use or to the use of any person not entitled to the use of the property."

Degrees, quoted:
> (2)(a): "If the property stolen is valued at $100,000 or more or is a semitrailer that was deployed by a law enforcement officer" -- felony of the first degree
> (2)(b): "If the property stolen is valued at $20,000 or more, but less than $100,000" -- felony of the second degree
> (2)(c): "Valued at $750 or more, but less than $5,000", "$5,000 or more, but less than $10,000", "$10,000 or more, but less than $20,000" -- felony of the third degree

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/775.082** (allowlisted). 2025.
> (3)(b) first degree felony: "by a term of imprisonment not exceeding 30 years or, when specifically provided by statute, by imprisonment for a term of years not exceeding life imprisonment"
> (3)(d) second degree felony: "by a term of imprisonment not exceeding 15 years"
> (3)(e) third degree felony: "by a term of imprisonment not exceeding 5 years"
> (10): "if the total sentence points pursuant to s. 921.0024 are 22 points or fewer, the court must sentence the offender to a nonstate prison sanction"

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/921.0024** (allowlisted). 2025.
> (2): "The lowest permissible sentence is any nonstate prison sanction in which the total sentence points equals or is less than 44 points."
> (2), where points exceed 44: "subtract 28 points from the total sentence points and decreasing the remaining total by 25 percent."

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/921.0022** (allowlisted), offense

grand theft is level 6; the same conduct charged as scheme to defraud is level
5. Charging choice moves the score. No page in this market says so, and it is
verified from the chart. That is the strongest single paragraph available to
this page.
