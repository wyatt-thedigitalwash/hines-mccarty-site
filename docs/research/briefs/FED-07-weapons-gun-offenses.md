## 0. THE OPEN QUESTION THIS BRIEF CLOSES

The pair verdict in OVERLAP-AUDIT was reached on the prohibited-person lists
alone. That is enough to keep two pages, but it is not enough to build one: a
page whose whole substance is "the federal list is longer" is a comparison, and
a comparison of two lists is the state page read from the other side.

**The question. Does the federal page have substance that exists nowhere on the
state page and does not depend on the state page for its meaning?**

**Answer: yes, and it is not the list.** It is the penalty architecture around
922(g), which has no Florida analogue at all. Three verified pieces:

| Piece | Statute | Verified this session |
|---|---|---|
| Fifteen years is the **maximum** for a 922(g) conviction | 18 U.S.C. 924(a)(8) | yes |
| Fifteen years is the **minimum** for the same conviction once three qualifying priors were committed on occasions different from one another | 18 U.S.C. 924(e)(1) | yes |
| A 924(c) count **cannot** run concurrently with any other term | 18 U.S.C. 924(c)(1)(D)(ii) | yes |

The first two are the same number on both ends of the same offense. Nothing in
the Florida 790 series does that, and the state brief's own differentiator, the
three-year minimum under F.S. 775.087, is a different mechanism pointing the
other way. **The prohibited-person contrast moves to the forum section, where
C10 wants it. The C9 substance is the ceiling-and-floor.**

## 5. The C9 differentiator

Ready to paste into `CHARGE_C9` in src/lib/site.ts under the key
`federal/weapons-gun-offenses`:

```
statute:   "18 U.S.C. 924"
section:   "The Armed Career Criminal Act"
assertion: "Fifteen years is the ceiling and the floor for the same conviction: 924(a)(8) caps a 922(g) violation at fifteen years, and 924(e)(1) makes fifteen years the minimum once three violent felony or serious drug convictions were committed on occasions different from one another."
```

**Why it is false or nonsensical if another charge is swapped in.** 924(e)
attaches to nothing except a violation of 922(g). Swap in wire fraud, money
laundering, healthcare fraud, or federal drug trafficking and the sentence is
not merely wrong, it has no referent: none of those statutes has a provision
that converts its own statutory maximum into its statutory minimum, and none of
them is reachable by ACCA. Swap in the state gun page and it is wrong for a
second, independent reason: there is no Florida provision that inverts a
maximum, and F.S. 775.087 works by adding a minimum on top of an unchanged
maximum, which is the opposite mechanism.

**Collision check, by hand.** `statuteKey` resolves this to `18 usc 924`. The
only other populated gun assertion is `state/gun-crimes-defense` at
`F.S. 775.087`, so the automated checker will not compare them, which is exactly
why the manual check matters. That assertion is a three-year minimum applying to
two named offenses as an exception to a ten-year rule. This one is a fifteen-year
figure appearing as both ends of a single offense. Different statutes, different
mechanisms, opposite directions. **No collision.** Checked also against
`federal/money-laundering` (predicate list and absence of thresholds) and
`state/drug-trafficking-defense` (weight tiers): no shared substance.

**Section 3 passes independently.** The nine-category list at 922(g) exists for
no other statute in either silo, so the page survives the cover-the-name test
even if section 7 were struck.
