## 0. THE OPEN QUESTION, AND THE VERDICT

**The question this brief had to answer.** The page's differentiator was
expected to rest on whether 21 U.S.C. 846 and 18 U.S.C. 1349 require an overt
act where 18 U.S.C. 371 does. That is a proposition with two halves, and only
one of them is a statutory-text question.

**Verdict: the statutory half is VERIFIED and it is enough. The case-law half is
not fetched and the page must not assert it.**

| Half | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 18 U.S.C. 371 states an overt act requirement on the face of its text | **Verified** | "and one or more of such persons do any act to effect the object of the conspiracy". Fetched, quoted in Section 6. |
| 21 U.S.C. 846, 18 U.S.C. 1349, and 18 U.S.C. 1956(h) contain no overt act language | **Verified as an absence** | All three are one sentence long and all three were fetched in full. The sentence is reproduced in Section 6. There is nowhere in the text for such a requirement to hide. |
| Courts have *held* that no overt act need be proved under 846 and 1349 | **UNVERIFIED. Do not assert.** | That is a judicial holding, not statutory text, and no allowlisted source for it was fetched. Section 6 records it and what to check. |

**How the page states it, and this distinction is load-bearing.** The page
describes what each statute says and does not say. It does not say "no overt act
is required" as a rule of law. Descriptively, one statute names an act and the
others name only the agreement and the penalty, and that difference is visible
to any reader who reads the three sentences side by side.

**The penalty contrast came in stronger than the overt act contrast, and it was
not what the page was expected to rest on.** Section 371 caps at five years
whatever the object offense was. The three chapter-specific conspiracy
provisions carry "the same penalties as those prescribed for the offense". All
four are verified. That contrast is the C9 in Section 5, with the overt act
point riding alongside it.

## 5. The C9 differentiator

Ready to populate the `c9` field directly:

```
statute:   "18 U.S.C. 371, against 21 U.S.C. 846 and 18 U.S.C. 1349"
section:   "Which conspiracy statute you are charged under"
assertion: "Section 371 names an act to effect the object of the conspiracy on the face of its text and caps the sentence at five years whatever the object offense was, while the conspiracy provisions at 21 U.S.C. 846 and 18 U.S.C. 1349 name only the agreement and carry the same penalties as the completed offense."
```

**Why it is false or nonsensical if another charge is swapped in.** Every clause
of the assertion is a statement about a conspiracy provision comparing itself to
another conspiracy provision. Swap in wire fraud and there is no five-year cap
and nothing to compare 1343 against on an overt act axis, because 1343 has no
agreement element at all. Swap in money laundering and the sentence describes
1956(h), which is a conspiracy provision, not the money laundering offense. Swap
in any substantive offense and the phrase "the same penalties as the completed
offense" becomes incoherent, because the substantive offense *is* the completed
offense. **The assertion is only meaningful for a charge whose penalty is
defined by reference to another charge, and conspiracy is the charge that is.**

**Collision check performed.** `statuteKey` in `src/lib/c9.ts` takes the first
citation match, so this resolves to `18 usc 371`. No other entry in `CHARGE_C9`
cites title 18 section 371, and the populated federal entry in that space is
`federal/money-laundering` at `18 usc 1956`. Checked by hand as well as by key:
that assertion rests on a predicate list and the absence of a dollar threshold,
this one rests on penalty-by-reference and an overt act clause. No overlap in
substance. **One live adjacency to watch:** 1956(h) is quoted here as a
supporting row, and `18 usc 1956` is the money laundering page's key. It appears
in this brief as one row of a four-row comparison and never as the page's
substance. If a drafter promotes it, the pages collide. Recorded so the check is
made at draft time, since the automated checker compares only the first citation
and would not see it.

## 6. Sources and verification status

### Verified this session, fetched 2026-08-13

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section371**
(allowlisted per D2). "Text contains those laws in effect on August 12, 2026."
Chapter 19, Conspiracy. Full section text, quoted:

> "If two or more persons conspire either to commit any offense against the United States, or to defraud the United States, or any agency thereof in any manner or for any purpose, and one or more of such persons do any act to effect the object of the conspiracy, each shall be fined under this title or imprisoned not more than five years, or both.
> If, however, the offense, the commission of which is the object of the conspiracy, is a misdemeanor only, the punishment for such conspiracy shall not exceed the maximum punishment provided for such misdemeanor."

Section title, verbatim: "Conspiracy to commit offense or to defraud United
States." Note that the title itself carries the two objects, which is worth the
H2 in outline item 5: **defrauding the United States is a distinct object from
committing an offense against it.**

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title21-section846**
(allowlisted). "Text contains those laws in effect on August 12, 2026." Title
21, chapter 13, subchapter I, part D. Section title "Attempt and conspiracy".
Full section text, quoted:

> "Any person who attempts or conspires to commit any offense defined in this subchapter shall be subject to the same penalties as those prescribed for the offense, the commission of which was the object of the attempt or conspiracy."

That is the entire section. **No overt act language is present**, and the 1988
amendment note on the same page shows the current penalty phrase replaced
"is punishable by imprisonment or fine or both which may not exceed the maximum
punishment prescribed for the offense", which changed a ceiling into an
equivalence. verified.

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1349**
(allowlisted). "Text contains those laws in effect on August 12, 2026." Title
18, chapter 63, Mail Fraud and Other Fraud Offenses. Section title "Attempt and
conspiracy". Full section text, quoted:

> "Any person who attempts or conspires to commit any offense under this chapter shall be subject to the same penalties as those prescribed for the offense, the commission of which was the object of the attempt or conspiracy."

Added by Pub. L. 107-204, title IX, section 902(a), July 30, 2002. Word for word
the same sentence as 846 with "this chapter" for "this subchapter". **No overt
act language.** verified.

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1956**
(allowlisted), subsection (h), quoted:

> "(h) Any person who conspires to commit any offense defined in this section or section 1957 shall be subject to the same penalties as those prescribed for the offense the commission of which was the object of the conspiracy."

Third instance of the same drafting pattern. **No overt act language.** verified.

**https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title21-section841**
(allowlisted), 841(b)(1)(A), the penalty 846 imports for the largest quantity
tier, quoted:

> "such person shall be sentenced to a term of imprisonment which may not be less than 10 years or more than life and if death or serious bodily injury results from the use of such substance shall be not less than 20 years or more than life, a fine not to exceed the greater of that authorized in accordance with the provisions of title 18 or $10,000,000 if the defendant is an individual or $50,000,000 if the defendant is other than an individual, or both."

This is the concrete half of the FAQ answer to "how serious is a conspiracy
charge". A 371 conspiracy tops out at five years. An 846 conspiracy to the same
tier as 841(b)(1)(A) starts at ten. **Use the quantity-tier framing carefully:
the tiers themselves belong to the drug trafficking page and this page cites the
penalty sentence only.**

**https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2025/777.04** (allowlisted:
flsenate.gov). 2025 Florida Statutes. Subsections (3) and (4)(a), quoted:

> "(3) A person who agrees, conspires, combines, or confederates with another person or persons to commit any offense commits the offense of criminal conspiracy, ranked for purposes of sentencing as provided in subsection (4)."
> "(4)(a) Except as otherwise provided in ss. 104.091(2), 379.2431(1), 828.125(2), 849.25(4), 893.135(5), and 921.0022, the offense of criminal attempt, criminal solicitation, or criminal conspiracy is ranked for purposes of sentencing under chapter 921 and determining incentive gain-time eligibility under chapter 944 one level below the ranking under s. 921.0022 or s. 921.0023 of the offense attempted, solicited, or conspired to. If the criminal attempt, criminal solicitation, or criminal conspiracy is of an offense ranked in level 1 or level 2 under s. 921.0022 or s. 921.0023, such offense is a misdemeanor of the first degree, punishable as provided in s. 775.082 or s. 775.083."

Subsections (4)(b) through (4)(f) were also fetched and set the degree ladder:
capital object gives a first degree felony, life or first degree object gives a
second degree felony, second degree object and certain third degree objects give
a third degree felony, other third degree objects give a first degree
misdemeanor. verified. **The Florida provision names the agreement and no act**,
which is the fourth data point in the pattern and makes the state-federal
contrast a penalty contrast rather than an elements contrast. Used in outline
item 4 in one or two sentences only, never as this page's substance, since this
is a federal page.
