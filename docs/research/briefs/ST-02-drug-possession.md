**Eight related searches. Every one of them is a statute string or a booking
description. Not one is a natural-language phrase.**

Two of them are not even statute citations, they are charge-description strings
in the exact register a Florida jail booking sheet or charging document uses:
`893.13 6a drugs possess cntrl sub wo prescription` and
`Drug equip possess and or use`. The second maps to F.S. 893.147(1), use or
possession with intent to use drug paraphernalia. The abbreviation "Fss" is
"Florida State Statute" as it is commonly typed by people copying from a
document, not by people who read statutes.

**What this tells us, and it is unusually direct evidence for a keyword
finding.** These are people holding a piece of paper, typing what it says into
Google, and trying to find out what they have been charged with. They are not
comparison shopping for a lawyer and they are not researching penalties in the
abstract. They arrive already charged.

## 4. The question set a person would ask an AI assistant

This is where the FAQ block comes from. Eight questions, written as a person
holding a charging document would phrase them.

1. "What does 893.13(6)(a) mean on my charging document?"
2. "How many grams of weed is a felony in Florida?"
3. "Is a THC cart or wax treated the same as flower in Florida?"
4. "The drugs were in the car, not on me. Can they still charge me?"
5. "I have a prescription for it but it was not in the bottle. Is that still possession?"
6. "Will I lose my driver license for a drug possession charge in Florida?"
7. "Can I get drug court on a possession charge?"
8. "What is the difference between possession and trafficking in Florida?"

**FAQ block: questions 2, 3, 5, and 6. Four questions.** Each is answerable
completely in two sentences from text quoted in Section 8, and each is
nonsensical about any other charge in the set.

- **Question 2 is the lead**, and it is the single most-searched fact on this
  page. The answer is the 20-gram line at 893.13(6)(b), and the useful half of
  it is that the line runs downward: 20 grams or less is a first degree
  misdemeanor, and everything above it falls back to the third degree felony in
  (6)(a).
- **Question 3 is the sharpest question on the list and it is where the statute
  genuinely surprises people.** 893.13(6)(b) closes with a definition that
  excludes "the resin extracted from the plants of the genus Cannabis, or any
  compound manufacture, salt, derivative, mixture, or preparation of such
  resin." **The 20-gram misdemeanor line does not reach concentrate at all**, so
  a quantity of resin that would be a misdemeanor as flower is not within (6)(b)
  by the subsection's own terms. Verified verbatim. No competitor page in the
  observed field carries this. Two sentences, then a link to the table.
- **Question 5 is the prescription element**, which is inside the offense
  definition rather than outside it: (6)(a) reaches possession "unless such
  controlled substance was lawfully obtained from a practitioner or pursuant to
  a valid prescription or order of a practitioner while acting in the course of
  his or her professional practice."
- **Question 6 is the licence question**, and it is the page's cleanest accuracy
  differentiator. F.S. 322.055(1), fetched and quoted: the court **shall** direct
  suspension, and the period is **6 months**, subject to evaluation and, if
  deemed necessary, completion of a drug treatment and rehabilitation program.
  The most-cited firm page on this query says "up to 1 year" and discretionary.
  The AI Overview says "6-month to 1-year." **Two sentences, both cited, both
  correcting the field.**

**Question 1 is the code-to-plain-English block and belongs in body copy**, not
the FAQ. It is Section 2.3's table and compressing it into two sentences would
throw away the page's best long-tail asset.

**Question 4 is constructive possession and belongs in the elements section.**
The statute's own words are "actual or constructive possession," so the page can
name the doctrine from the operative text. **What it must not do is state a test
for constructive possession**, because no element test was fetched and the
governing test is case law rather than statute. See Section 8. Describe what the
state is asserting and what the contested questions are (whose vehicle, whose
residence, who had access), which is the same process-only framing W2-02 used
for its constructive possession paragraph and which plan Section 12 requires.

**Question 7 is drug court and ships qualified.** F.S. 948.08(6)(b) was fetched
and quoted, and it makes a person eligible for voluntary admission into a
pretrial substance abuse education and treatment intervention program, including
a treatment-based drug court program under s. 397.334, on four conditions
including that the person "is charged with a nonviolent felony." **The step from
that to "a possession charge qualifies" runs through the definition of forcible
felony in s. 776.08, which was not fetched.** See Section 8. Until it is, the
page states what the statute provides and does not characterise this charge's
eligibility. It never suggests an outcome, per plan Section 12 and Rule
4-7.13(b)(3).

**Question 8 is the boundary with the sibling page** and is handled in Section 6.
It gets one paragraph and one link, and it is not an FAQ, because a complete
answer names two statutes and would not fit two sentences honestly.

### The cover-the-name test

Cover the words "drug possession" and read section 4 of the outline. The table
reads: one statutory paragraph, two offense levels, two point totals, one
statutory maximum. Then a first degree felony that is reached by ten grams.

- **Swap in drug trafficking** and it is false in every particular. Trafficking
  is a first degree felony in every tier, so the degree never varies, and W2-02
  verified that no trafficking tier is ranked below level 7. There is no
  paragraph of 893.135 that appears twice in the chart at two levels, and there
  is no trafficking offense at level 1 or level 3.
- **Swap in grand theft** and the ladder becomes property value, from the $750
  felony threshold to the $100,000 first degree tier, and each subsection of
  812.014 maps to one level.
- **Swap in money laundering** and it becomes $300, $20,000, and $100,000 across
  a rolling 12 months.
- **Swap in unauthorized computer access** and 815.06 has no row in the chart at
  all, which is the constraint ST-01 was built around.
- **Swap in battery, DUI, or any other charge in the set** and there is no
  statutory paragraph anywhere in this project that has been observed to occupy
  two severity levels.

**The sentence is true of exactly one page.** Every other Florida ladder verified
in this project moves the degree and the level together. This one holds the
degree still and moves the level.
