## 0. THE OPEN QUESTION, AND THE VERDICT

This page carries the highest verified search volume in the entire project,
`tax evasion` at 880 per month at Florida level, more than double the next
largest term. The open question is whether that volume is reachable.

**Verdict: the volume is real, the commercial query is not winnable, and the
page should be built anyway, targeted at the informational term.**

Three findings force that split, all verified 2026-08-13.

1. **The commercial SERP belongs to a different industry.** The local pack on
   `tax evasion attorney Tampa` is Frost Law, J. David Tax Law LLC, and Law
   Offices of Darrin T. Mish, P.A. All three are tax controversy and IRS
   resolution practices, not criminal defense firms. The top three organic
   results are tampataxrelief.com, askfrost.com, and thedewittfirm.com, all
   IRS relief pages. **This is the only SERP observed anywhere in this project
   whose local pack contains zero criminal defense firms.** A criminal defense
   page is not competing against thin criminal defense pages here. It is
   competing against a funded tax resolution vertical whose acquisition
   economics are built on collections work, not on defense retainers.
2. **The related searches confirm the intent split and it does not favour us.**
   Every related search returned is tax controversy or navigational: `Tax
   attorney Tampa`, `Tax attorney near me`, `DeWitt Law Firm Tampa`, `Tampa
   tenant lawyer`, `Real estate lawyer Tampa`, `Dewitt tax law`, `DeWitt Law
   Firm Orlando`, `Tax attorney Florida`. Not one of them is a defense query.
3. **The one question the SERP asks that nobody in it can answer is the one
   this page owns.** People Also Ask carries "What is the penalty for tax
   evasion in Florida?" The honest answer is that Florida does not criminalise
   evading a tax on personal income at all, and its only criminal tax offence
   is something structurally different. That answer is verified in Section 6,
   it is the C9, and no tax resolution firm in the SERP has any reason to write
   it.

**What follows for the build.** Do not sequence this page on the commercial
term. Build it for the informational Florida-level term and for the assistant
question set, and measure it there, which is the treatment
CASES-WE-DEFEND-PLAN.md Section 7 and TEMPLATE-FINDINGS Section 5 already
prescribe for waves 1 and 2 generally. This page is the strongest instance of
that rule in the set, not an exception to it.

**One limb is unmet.** The constitutional basis for "Florida has no personal
income tax," Fla. Const. Art. VII s. 5, failed three fetch attempts. The C9
does not depend on it. See Section 6.

## 5. The C9 differentiator

Ready to paste into the `c9` field in `src/lib/site.ts`:

```
statute:   "26 U.S.C. 7201 and F.S. 212.15(2)"
section:   "Forum section: is there a Florida tax evasion charge?"
assertion: "Florida's only criminal tax offense punishes failing to remit sales tax already collected from a customer, a theft of state funds rather than an evasion, and the one Florida statute that uses the words attempt to evade or defeat tax, F.S. 213.29, imposes a civil penalty of twice the tax instead of a crime."
```

**Why it is false or nonsensical if another charge is swapped in.** Every clause
is tied to a statute that exists nowhere else in the set. No other charge has a
Florida analogue that is a civil penalty provision. No other charge's Florida
version punishes failing to hand over money collected from a third party rather
than the defendant's own conduct toward the defendant's own obligation. Swap in
money laundering and the sentence is false in both limbs: F.S. 896.101 is
criminal, it is not about remittance, and Florida's version is not civil. Swap
in grand theft and the second clause is meaningless, because no statute in the
theft chapter uses the phrase "attempt to evade or defeat tax." The assertion
cannot survive the substitution of any other charge name.

**Collision check.** `statuteKey` in `src/lib/c9.ts` matches the USC pattern
first, so this resolves to `26 usc 7201`. No populated assertion in
`CHARGE_C9` rests on any title 26 statute, and none rests on F.S. 212.15 or
F.S. 213.29. Checked by hand as well as by key, because the plan's Section 4.5
collision arrived through a door the key check does not watch:

| Sibling assertion | Substance | Collides? |
|---|---|---|
| `white-collar/wire-fraud`, F.S. 817.034 | No Florida offence carries the name, the analogue is the Communications Fraud Act | **No, but adjacent.** Both involve a Florida analogue with a different name. The difference is load-bearing: wire fraud's analogue is a criminal statute with its own aggregate-value tiers, this page's analogue is a civil penalty plus a theft statute about someone else's money. **The plan's copy-level variation requirement in Section 4.3 applies. This page must not open with the same "no Florida offence by that name" sentence shape.** Recorded so a drafter checks the two side by side. |
| `white-collar/money-laundering`, F.S. 896.101 | Three-tier ladder from $300 across a rolling 12 months, level 7 floor | No. Opposite structure and opposite ranking outcome. |
| `white-collar/embezzlement`, F.S. 812.014 | Possession lawful when it began | No. Different statute, different element. |
| `white-collar/identity-theft`, F.S. 817.568 | Protected-person and custodial tiers | No. |
| `state/grand-theft-defense`, F.S. 812.014 | Value ladder | No. |

**A second differentiator sits behind the first, and it is stronger than
expected.** F.S. 212.15(2) appears in the F.S. 921.0022 ranking chart exactly
once, at level 1, for the third degree tier only. The second degree and first
degree tiers are not in the chart, so under F.S. 921.0023 they sit at levels 4
and 7 by default. That is the reverse of the money laundering finding in
W2-05, where every tier was ranked and ranked high. It is not the named C9,
because the named one is more legible to a reader, but it is real, verified,
and invisible on every competitor page in this market.
