## 2. Tactics That Risk Florida Bar Problems

Every OBSERVED-NOT-RECOMMENDED item across the six files, consolidated and
deduplicated, grouped by rule. These are observed competitor patterns and design
temptations; none is an adjudicated violation, and none should be copied.

### 2a. Unverifiable superiority claims (Rule 4-7.13)

14. **"Top" / "Best" in titles and slugs.** Hanlon's "Top Tampa Criminal Defense
    Attorney" title, mjpetro's "Tampa's Best Mail Fraud and Wire Fraud Defense
    Attorney," allcriminaldefense.law's "Top Criminal Defense Lawyers Near Me,"
    Rickman's "Best Federal Defense Attorney," dempslaw's stacked
    "Top...Expert...best" Wesley Chapel title, and Brancato's
    /top-rated-tampa-homicide-attorney/ slugs are all objectively unverifiable
    superiority claims. [competitors.md Sec. 2 Cluster C and 3.5/3.7; keywords.md
    Sec. 2 and Sec. 5, all verified in SERP titles or on-page]
15. **Importing directory listicle superlatives into the firm's own copy.** A
    "Best Criminal Lawyers in Tampa" directory headline is the directory's claim,
    not the firm's, and republishing it as self-description converts it into a
    4-7.13 problem. [local-authority.md Sec. 5.2 item 6]
16. **Paulk-style self-laudatory copy.** "We are heavyweights in the courtroom"
    and "one of Florida's leading criminal defense lawyers" are unverifiable
    quality and comparative characterizations. [competitors.md Sec. 3.6, verified
    on fetched pages]
17. **Puffery-adjacent "best" phrasing.** Brunvand's "you want the best legal
    team possible" and Rankin's clients "receive the best possible defense" ride
    the 4-7.13 line even when arguably puffery; a new firm gains nothing from
    testing that line. [competitors.md Sec. 3.1 and 3.2, verified on-page]

### 2b. Expert/specialist language absent board certification (Rule 4-7.14)

18. **Firm-level "expert" claims.** Whittel & Melton's "expert, experienced, and
    focused representation" while only one unnamed attorney is certified is
    exactly the phrasing the Bar has flagged. [competitors.md Sec. 3.3, verified
    on-page]
19. **"Specializes in" without matching certification.** Brunvand's "working with
    a lawyer who specializes in DUI charges" (certification is criminal trial
    law, not DUI) and Metcalf Falls' live ad creative "specialize in criminal
    defense and DUI charges" both implicate 4-7.14. [competitors.md Sec. 3.2;
    paid-reality.md Sec. 5, verified creative]
20. **"Expert" in ad creatives and URL slugs.** Curry's "Injury Experts on Your
    Side" creative, Brancato's /expert-florida-trial-strategy.../ slug, and
    rogolaw's "Expert Criminal Defense in Plant City" title are gray-to-risky
    absent certification; Hines & McCarty credential language is gated on
    STATE.md Q5. [paid-reality.md Sec. 5; competitors.md Sec. 3.7; keywords.md
    Sec. 5 Tier 1]

### 2c. Results presented without required disclaimers (Rules 4-7.13(b), 4-7.15)

21. **Case-results pages with no results-may-vary disclaimer.** Paulk's
    case-results page shows "All Charges Dismissed" outcomes with a verified
    absence of any disclaimer, a direct 4-7.13(b)(2) exposure. [competitors.md
    Sec. 3.6]
22. **Result-quoting testimonials without disclaimers.** Hanlon's practice pages
    and reviews.html quote "all three charges dismissed" style outcomes with no
    visible disclaimer; a review mentioning results needs the clear and
    conspicuous "may not obtain the same or similar results" language quoted in
    the 2025 Handbook. [competitors.md Sec. 3.5; local-authority.md Sec. 5.2
    item 3, Handbook verified]
23. **Disclaimer-free presentation of large results sets.** Rankin's ~50
    specific results with no proximate disclaimer and Brancato's ~80 anonymized
    results without case numbers (not independently verifiable as presented) are
    formats to run through compliance review, not copy. [competitors.md Sec. 3.1
    and 3.7]
24. **Aggregate win claims.** Oberheiden's "2,000+ Cases Won" plus media-logo
    wall and Bark's "defended thousands of Florida felony cases" are record
    characterizations that must be objectively verifiable under 4-7.13(b);
    do not imitate. [content-gaps.md item 7; tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 4
    OBSERVED-NOT-RECOMMENDED]
25. **Review-count-as-headline titles.** Stroleny's "400+ 5-Star Reviews" title
    tag is self-asserted and unchecked against testimonial rules; verify against
    4-7.13 before ever leading with an aggregate review count. [competitors.md
    Sec. 2 Cluster C; keywords.md Sec. 2 Cluster C]

### 2d. Misleading names, domains, and framing (Rules 4-7.13, 4-7.21)

26. **Outcome-promising domain names.** Brunvand's acquitter.com implies an
    achievable result in the domain itself, a tactic 4-7.13's
    results-characterization prohibition makes unavailable to a new firm.
    [competitors.md Sec. 3.2]
27. **Plural "Offices"/"attorneys" branding for a solo presentation.** Paulk's
    "Law Offices of Jeff Paulk"/"attorneys" branding with one visible lawyer is
    Rule 4-7.21 territory. [competitors.md Sec. 3.6]
28. **Fear-marketing urgency copy.** Evergreen's "last best chance" target-letter
    framing is the pattern to avoid on pre-charge pages; urgency must stay
    factual (deadlines, locations, recorded-line warnings). [content-gaps.md
    item 6; tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 2.4]
29. **Unsourced scare statistics.** Paulk's unsourced "1.5 million false or
    trivial restraining orders / nearly 70%" claim and his internally
    inconsistent 4,500+ vs 7,000+ case counts show how unvetted numbers become
    potentially misleading statements. [competitors.md Sec. 3.6, verified
    inconsistency]
30. **Legal-accuracy overstatements.** Scriven's claim that expungement purges
    the record "from all databases including law enforcement databases"
    overstates F.S. 943.0585, and Erase The Case's "3-5 months vs industry 5-7"
    speed comparison is unvetted; both are accuracy/4-7.13 traps in the
    expungement niche. [content-gaps.md Topic 8 and item 19]
31. **"Confidential" labels on marketing tools.** Bark's "Free, Confidential
    Tool" SERP title risks implying attorney-client confidentiality that does
    not exist for an anonymous calculator. [tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 4
    OBSERVED-NOT-RECOMMENDED]
32. **"First in Florida" tool marketing.** Interactive FL scoresheet calculators
    already exist (Bark, Delgado, communitylawfirm), so a first-mover claim
    would be simply false under 4-7.13. [tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 1 correction;
    keywords.md Sec. 4 correction; STATE.md]
33. **Advertising capabilities the firm does not have.** "Se habla espanol"
    without genuine Spanish capability, "Available 24/7" without staffed
    answering, and implying a Tampa office before a bona fide Tampa office
    exists (4-7.12 comment, primary text verified) are all misleading
    communications. [keywords.md Sec. 6; paid-reality.md Sec. 5 and Sec. 6
    item 10]

### 2e. Review-program violations (Rules 4-7.13(b)(9), 4-7.17(b), 4-7.18)

34. **Incentivized reviews.** Flatly prohibited by two Handbook-verified rules
    ("the person making the testimonial may receive nothing of value" and "a
    lawyer may not give anything of value to a person for recommending"), plus
    Google and FTC policy; the program must be ask-only. [local-authority.md
    Sec. 5.2 item 2, verified quotes]
35. **Drafting or suggesting review wording.** The lawyer may not write or draft
    the testimonial, so never send suggested language with an ask.
    [local-authority.md Sec. 5.2 item 3]
36. **Review requests to non-clients.** Asks to people who are not
    current/former clients or prior professional contacts can trigger the
    direct-solicitation regime of Rule 4-7.18(b). [local-authority.md Sec. 5.2
    item 4, inferred extension noted there]
37. **Republishing matter-referencing reviews without consent.** Informed
    consent under Rule 4-1.6(a) is required before reposting a review that
    references a client matter. [local-authority.md Sec. 5.2 item 3]

### 2f. Tool and intake design traps (Op. 24-1, Rule 4-1.18)

38. **Lead-capture gates on tool results.** Capturing identity or case facts
    before showing calculator output triggers prospective-client duties under
    Rule 4-1.18 and confidentiality safeguards; the compliance-conservative
    design is anonymous inputs with ungated results. [tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 4
    items 9-10; STATE.md Q12]
39. **Shipping a calculator on a stale formula.** An outdated scoresheet
    computation is a materially inaccurate statement under 4-7.13(a)(1), so the
    Rule 3.992 revision implemented must be date-stamped and kept synced or the
    tool should not ship. [tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 4 items 1-2]

## 5. Ad-Platform Restrictions for Lawyers (supplemental check, new this pass)

Run this pass against primary policy pages; two Google fetches and one Meta fetch
succeeded, one Meta URL 404'd before the correct page was found (strike recorded,
resolved).

49. **Google Ads prohibits bail bonds promotion outright.** "Promotion of bail
    bonds services that offer to act as surety in order to secure bail for a
    defendant is not allowed" (complete prohibition, not a certification
    program), so any bail-bonds-adjacent ad angle, co-marketing funnel, or
    landing page positioned around securing bail as a surety service is
    unbuyable on Google; criminal defense legal services themselves are not
    restricted on this page (verified, fetched 2026-07-22).
    Source: https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6368711
50. **Google personalized-ads policy blocks remarketing for this practice
    area.** "Commission of a crime" is a listed sensitive interest category, and
    advertisers in sensitive categories "are unable to use advertiser-curated
    audiences," so do not plan remarketing lists, customer-match uploads, or
    site-visitor retargeting for criminal defense campaigns; budget for
    search/LSA intent capture only (verified quote of the audience restriction,
    fetched 2026-07-22; the detailed sub-definitions of the category sit on
    linked pages not fetched, gap recorded).
    Source: https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465
51. **Meta prohibits bail bonds ads.** Bail bonds ("when a criminal defendant is
    granted conditional release from custody by paying a sum of money determined
    by the court") appear in Meta's prohibited financial products ad standard,
    while lawyer and legal services ads are not restricted anywhere on that page
    (verified, fetched 2026-07-22); a broader sweep of Meta ad standards for any
    lawyer-specific rule was not run within the fetch budget, so treat "Meta
    imposes no lawyer-specific ad restriction" as unconfirmed (gap recorded).
    Source: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/deceptive-content/prohibited-financial-products-and-services/
52. **LSA category constraints are prerequisites, not blockers.** Google's own
    screening pages (fetched by W6) confirm "Criminal lawyer" and "DUI lawyer"
    LSA categories exist nationwide with no state restriction, but require bar
    license checks for every lawyer in the firm plus professional liability
    insurance for the Google Screened badge, so do not attempt LSA onboarding
    before licenses and insurance documentation are in order. [paid-reality.md
    Sec. 3.1-3.2, verified via support.google.com/localservices answers 12174778
    and 7527305; STATE.md Q13]

## 7. Everything Else: Stale-Data Traps, Tool Dead Ends, Premature Plays

### 7a. Stale-data and bad-number traps (do not plan on these figures)

55. **Pareto Legal's "$659 cost per signed case."** Fails its own arithmetic
    ($60 CPL at 2% close is $3,000/case) and comes from a plaintiff-side study;
    excluded from planning. [paid-reality.md Sec. 1 and 2.3]
56. **WordStream/LocaliQ legal CPL $131.63 as fresh data.** Byte-identical
    across the 2025 and 2026 reports (likely unrefreshed) and both reports are
    one data pipeline, not two independent sources. [paid-reality.md Sec. 1]
57. **Keyword-Planner-style bid figures ($43-61/click) as expected CPCs.**
    Measured actual-paid data runs 3-5x lower; treat bid estimates as ceilings
    only, and treat agency sales-page narratives ($50-160 DUI, $300+ extreme)
    as uncited marketing. [paid-reality.md Sec. 1 and Sec. 6 items 1-2]
58. **W2's original "no FL calculator exists" finding.** Superseded by W5's
    verified discovery of the Bark and Delgado calculators; any strategy doc or
    pitch built on the first-mover premise is working from stale data.
    [tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 1 correction; keywords.md Sec. 4]
59. **Aggregator review counts taken as current.** Cross-aggregator
    discrepancies show a 5-25 percent staleness band, so never cite a
    TrustAnalytica/Birdeye number as a live Maps count without a geo-audit.
    [local-authority.md Sec. 1 item 2, verified from the discrepancies]
60. **FAQ schema sold as a SERP-feature play.** Google deprecated FAQ
    rich-result display for most sites since 2023, so schema value is
    entity/eligibility signaling, not visible rich results; do not promise the
    client stars-and-dropdowns. [content-gaps.md cross-topic finding 2]

### 7b. Tool dead ends (W5 verdicts)

61. **Federal sentencing guidelines range estimator: DO NOT BUILD.** The
    regulator ships a free official app and sentencing.us already holds the
    .edu links, leaving no differentiation path at the highest effort and
    highest accuracy risk of any candidate. [tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 2.2 and
    Sec. 3 item 6]
62. **Standalone charge-lookup tool: do not build separately.** The literal
    "charge lookup" head term pulls arrest-records intent (wrong audience) and
    real demand arrives per-statute, so the lookup belongs inside the CPC
    calculator as its input layer plus statute spoke pages. [tools-and-hooks.md
    Sec. 2.3]
63. **Standalone bail/bond walkthrough: do not build standalone.** The consumer
    "bail someone out" SERP is a bail-bondsman wall whose traffic a law firm
    cannot convert, and the attorney-convertible slice folds into the
    first-24-hours checklist. [tools-and-hooks.md Sec. 2.5]

### 7c. Premature plays (right idea, wrong order)

64. **Launching LSA before roughly 35+ Google reviews and genuine 24/7
    answering.** Review count and responsiveness are Google-documented LSA
    ranking inputs and missed calls damage rank, so a premature launch buys
    poor placement at full lead prices. [paid-reality.md Sec. 3.5 and Sec. 6
    item 7]
65. **Spanish pages, "se habla espanol," or the abogado.com/elAbogado buys
    before language capability is confirmed.** The Spanish SERP is real but
    contested, and advertising the capability without it is a 4-7.13 problem
    that also destroys intake conversion; if no capability exists, SKIP
    entirely. [keywords.md Sec. 6; local-authority.md Sec. 3.1; STATE.md Q8]
66. **Aggregator citation submission before the Brandon NAP is final.**
    Propagating then correcting a wrong name/address/phone across data
    aggregators is the most expensive citation mistake available; the office
    address (STATE.md Q3) gates the entire sequence. [local-authority.md
    Sec. 4.2 sequencing rule]
67. **Broad Tampa head-term search campaigns before conversion infrastructure
    is proven.** The organic SERP is directory-walled, bid ceilings are the
    highest in the market, and volume incumbents already sit there; paid should
    buy Brandon geography and arrest-adjacent moments first. [paid-reality.md
    Sec. 4.2 and Sec. 6 item 3]
68. **White-collar and federal paid as year-one pillars.** Single-source CPCs,
    low volume, and referral-moated organic argue for a capped ~$500/mo
    federal-moment test at most, killed fast if the thin-auction hypothesis
    fails. [paid-reality.md Sec. 4.1 item 4 and Sec. 6 item 4]
69. **Drug-trafficking-specific paid terms.** No published CPC exists anywhere

70. **Trying to out-spend Smith & Eulo's ~200-ad statewide play.** The counter
    to a statewide volume advertiser is Brandon depth and LSA, not matching
    breadth. [paid-reality.md Sec. 3.4 and Sec. 6 item 9]
71. **The medium budget scenario before small-scenario data lands.** 60-90 days
    of small-scenario spend replaces every CPC estimate in the dossier with the
    firm's own Tampa numbers; committing $6,000-8,000/mo before that is buying
    blind. [paid-reality.md Sec. 4.3]
72. **Building sealing/expungement content early.** It is the most crowded and
    lowest-opportunity of W3's eight topics (Sammis current, Erase The Case's

73. **Publishing Hillsborough bond-schedule dollar amounts before
